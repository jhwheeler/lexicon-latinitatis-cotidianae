# Lexicon editing instructions

## Source of truth

The authoritative lexicon is the structured data in `data/` on the `main` branch of this repository:
https://github.com/jhwheeler/lexicon-latinitatis-cotidianae

Published site:
https://jhwheeler.github.io/lexicon-latinitatis-cotidianae/

For any request to read, extend, correct, or edit the lexicon, fetch the current repository version first. New words go in as concept records in `data/concepts/` (one record per sense, with notes for every departure from Lewis & Short or a source, and rulings in `data/rulings.json`; see `docs/records.md`). Every grouped topic card in `data/entries/` was converted to records by 9 November 2026 (`scripts/convert_cards.py`, rules in `docs/converting.md`); the converted records are drafts until reviewed. Category metadata is in `data/catalog.json`. Page layout, styles and search live in `src/`. **Commit sources only: the site is not kept in the repository** (Jack, 15 November 2026; until then about 16,000 generated files were committed with every change). `python3 scripts/build.py` validates the data and writes the site beside the sources for looking at and testing (`index.html`, `k/`, `c/`, `l/`, `words.html`, `latin.html`, the guidance and practice pages, `sitemap.xml`, `manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js`, `assets/`); git ignores all of it, and a file written there by hand is overwritten. On every push to `main` the workflow `.github/workflows/pages.yml` builds the site afresh (`build.py --out _site`) and publishes that build; GitHub Pages takes the site from GitHub Actions, not from a branch. A new generated file at the top of the site needs a line in `.gitignore` (a test checks it). Run `python3 scripts/build.py`, `python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v`, `python3 scripts/check_quantities.py` (vowel lengths against Lewis & Short; a deliberate departure needs a note, a ruling or an entry in `data/quantity-exceptions.json`) and the browser search tests (`npm test`; setup in README.md) before committing; CI runs all four. `npm test` builds the site first. After a push to `main`, see that the run of "Publish the site" succeeded: that is the deployment. See `docs/data-format.md` for the catalog and `docs/records.md` for records.

## Latin orthography: consonantal i is j

Jack's decision (29 October 2026): the lexicon follows the orthography of the Theodōrus et pater readers and writes consonantal i as j in all Latin: headwords, phrases, examples and quotations (quotations are normalised to it).

- *jam, ejus, cujus, major, Troja, Jēsus, Jōannēs* (with long *ō* since 9 November 2026, after Lewis & Short's *Jōannes*; Jack).
- No macron on the vowel before intervocalic j (*major, ejus, pejus*: the vowel is short, the syllable heavy). Long prefixes keep theirs (*dējectus, prōjēcit, ējēcī*).
- Compounds: *abjicit, objice, dējiciō, subjēcit, conjungō, conjūnx, adjuvō*.
- i stays a vowel in the interjection *iō*, in forms of *eō* (*iērunt, adiērunt*), in *superior, interior, Plēiades*, in *Iūlus* where it scans as three syllables, after *qu/gu* (*quiēs, reliquiae*), and wherever it is a vowel (*diēs, cliēns, audiō, gaudium*).
- Only Latin changes. English, text in other languages and link targets keep their spelling; citation labels give the dictionary's own headword (Lewis & Short prints *janua, jus* but *deicio*).

After adding or editing Latin, run `python3 scripts/consonantal_j.py --list` and review both the proposed changes and the kept candidates, then `--write` and rebuild. `--check`, also run by the tests, fails while any Latin still needs j; record a genuine exception in the script's `KEEP` list with its reason. Content ported from older branches (written with i) needs this pass. Search treats i and j alike, so *iam* finds *jam*, and u and v alike, so *uideō* finds *videō*. Consonantal u is written v in the data; the display options can show it as u (lower case only: *uideō*, but *Venus*) as they can show j as i (Jack, 11 November 2026).

## Examples from the Theodōrus et pater readers

The readers (the libellī, and the cookbook that goes with them) are Jack's own and unpublished; whether they will be published is undecided. A reader of the lexicon cannot be expected to know them as he knows Cicero, so the site never cites them (Jack, 13 November 2026):

- In the data an example taken from them keeps its place as its `source`: `Libellus III, <span lang="la">Apud avum et aviam</span>`, or `<span lang="la">Culīna Theodōrī</span>` for the cookbook. The site shows it as "composed", like our other sentences, and the search does the same. A record's source line that names the readers (it begins *Theodōrus et pater*) stays in the data and is not shown.
- Prose never leans on them: no "the word of the libellī", "as in Libellus VII", "the series spelling". Say what is true without them: "our household word", "the house spelling", "a description of ours". Do not give a libellus as a term's `evidence`.
- In the readers *fābula* means only the tales told inside them, so it is not used for the readers themselves (Jack, 30 October 2026).

Examples kept this way quote the text word for word, and the readers are still being revised, so quotations drift. Whenever the readers change, run `python3 scripts/check_libelli.py --latinitas <path to a latinitas checkout>`: it checks every such example against the current texts and each title against the reader's `@title`, and lists looser mentions to look over. Re-quote what it reports from the current text and correct the English to match. Household words follow the series bible in the latinitas repository (pyjamas are *vestis nocturna*, not Traupman's *synthesis dormītōria*).

## Languages: English and Latin

Jack's decision (8 November 2026): the lexicon is English–Latin. Don't add glosses, labels, headings or search terms in Bulgarian or any other language by default. Cite another language only where it helps to explain a Latin word's meaning or form, as a cf. (for example, Lithuanian *ūdra* and Russian *vydra* in the ruling on *lutra*). Describe local things in English (white brined cheese, not *sirene*).

## Design

Jack's choice (9 November 2026): the "Minium" design. The rule is "say the black, do the red": Latin and its English are ink, and the rubrics (section heads, periods, note numbers, the control in use) are red. The text face is Gentium Book Plus, subset and renamed Lexicon Serif as its licence requires, with its own small capitals. Alegreya Sans is used only for controls. The layout uses rules, not boxes or pills (the one exception is the practice game's round: see Practice), and rules that run one above the other end at the same edges: the search has a single rule, under the field and Clear alike. Latin quoted in English prose (notes, rulings, comments) is italic.

Light, black and red on white, is the default. Jack found red on black hard to read (9 November 2026), so the dark theme is warm grey with cream ink: its headings and labels are cream, and red is kept to the small marks (note numbers, underlines, the control in use), as a soft rose. In the stylesheet, `--rubric` colours headings and labels and `--accent` the marks; in light both are red.

The lexicon has a third colour, lapis blue, the rubricator's other ink (15 November 2026): `--lapis:#24457a` with the tint `--lapis-soft:#e1e8f3` in light, `--lapis:#9db4d6` with `--lapis-soft:#2a3345` in dark; deep and a little grey in light, pale in dark, never a bright blue. It is the coordinating session's choice, made when Jack asked for a harmonious third colour for the clock, and is for Jack to judge. So far it is used only in the clock game (see Practice), and is defined only there. A future use would be right where a second kind of mark is needed and red is taken; it is not for text, headings or decoration, and it does not replace red.

The front page has the search and the contents (Jack, 9 November 2026). The categories run from the person outward (Jack, 13 November 2026; until then they stood in the order they had been added, playground first): conversation, common words and time; the body and clothes; the household, the house and the kitchen; nature and food, the town, travel; play and school; work, money, tools, building and computing; civic life and the church. The order is that of `filters` in `data/catalog.json`; a new category goes where it belongs in it, not at the end. Every page is linkable and works without JavaScript (Jack, 9 November 2026): each category has its own page (`k/<id>.html`) listing its entries from A to Z, with the categories beside it (on a phone, in a menu above), and `words.html` and `latin.html` list every headword by letter. The search, on the front page and the category pages, is the one part that needs scripts; its address keeps the query (`?q=`), and without scripts the lists stand in for it. Links to the old single page (`index.html#culina`, `#entry-528`) are redirected by `assets/legacy.js`. On a concept page the reasons (period, evidence, notes) stand in the margin beside the line they explain, and on a phone they fold under it. The header of every page is the name on the left, a search field in the middle and one settings control, "Aa", on the right, which opens the display options (macrons shown or hidden, consonantal i as j or i, consonantal u as v or u) and the theme (Jack, 11 November 2026). The header's field is a plain form that opens the front page with `?q=`, so it sends without scripts; the front page and the category pages, which search in place, show only their own field. On a phone the field takes its own line under the name, always in view. Pressing `/` focuses the search. How we decide, Rulings and About are in the footer of every page, not the header; the settings need scripts and are hidden without them. The site must work well on a phone: nothing scrolls sideways, every control is at least 44px tall, and the search field's text is at least 16px. The browser tests check all three. Opened from the home screen of an iPhone, a scrolled page showed through under the status bar (Jack, 14 November 2026): `body::before` is a strip of paper of the bar's height (`env(safe-area-inset-top)`) fixed above everything, and whatever sticks to the top of the screen sticks at that height, not at 0. This was written without a phone to try it on. The fonts and their licences are in `src/fonts/` (see its README).

## Speed, the app and offline use

Jack's requirements (13 November 2026): the search must feel instant, with no backend, and the lexicon must open from his phone's home screen like an app, without an app store.

- The search draws 30 results at a time, with "Show 30 more" under them; the count line gives the full number. Never draw every match: a single letter matches thousands of entries, and drawing them all took seconds on a phone. The search data is fetched when the page is idle after loading, so it is ready before the first letter; while it is on its way the status line says "Loading the lexicon…" and the rule under the field moves.
- `manifest.webmanifest`, the icons (`src/icons/`, drawn by `scripts/make_icons.mjs`) and the tags `app_head` puts in every page make "Add to Home Screen" open the lexicon in its own window, not a browser tab.
- The service worker (`src/sw.js`, built to `sw.js` at the root) keeps the front page, the category pages, the guidance pages, the practice pages and their cards, the stylesheet, the scripts, the search data, the fonts and the icons on the device when it installs, and the entry and word pages as they are read (the 600 most recent). The lexicon then opens at once and searches offline. Its version is a hash of the files it saves, so every build that changes them makes browsers fetch them afresh: a reader sees a change at the second opening after it is published, not the first. Do not give the saved files names that change, and do not add a second cache for them.
- The browser tests cover the batches, the loading state, the manifest and offline use.

## Words or phrases

Jack's request (13 November 2026): the category lists and both A–Z indexes have a filter, All / Words / Phrases, so the sayings can be browsed by themselves and the words are not buried under them. A record is a phrase when its `pos` is `phrase` (or includes it); give every saying or sentence that part of speech. The choice is kept on the device and carries from one list to the next. The Latin index lists the Latin of every phrase record beside the words, so phrases can be found from their Latin. The front page links to the A–Z indexes above the categories.

## Every Latin word linked

Jack's request (13 November 2026): every word of the lexicon's Latin leads to its entry, and every word used has one. The builder links each word of a term and of an example to the page of the word it is a form of (*potest* to *possum*, *casā* to *casa*), reading the forms from the dictionary forms in the data with their macrons; see "Every Latin word linked" in `docs/records.md`.

- Give every term its full dictionary form, correctly marked: the genitive and gender of a noun, all the principal parts of a verb. A form the dictionary form does not show is not found, and a macron that differs between an example and its entry breaks the link.
- A form that could belong to several words is linked only when the choice is safe, and otherwise left alone. Never loosen that to raise the count: a wrong link is worse than none. The rules are in `FormIndex.choose` in `scripts/build.py`; the ratio by which a commoner word may take a form (`LINK_RATIO`) and the quiet style of the links in examples (the class `tap`) are Claude's choices, for Jack to judge.
- After adding records, run `python3 scripts/latin_unlinked.py` and read `notes/coverage/unlinked.md`: the words without an entry are the list for new records, and the words "found only when the macrons are ignored" are usually slips to correct.

## Coinages only where needed

Jack's principle (13 November 2026): a word or phrase we came up with (a term whose `period` is `ours`) belongs in the lexicon only when it is needed: when no author of any period has a word for the concept, or a circumlocution has a really good reason. Otherwise take the word from earlier authors; they need not be classical, though the closer to Cicero the more weight. The modern reference works count as sources (Traupman's *Conversational Latin*, Morgan–Owens, Levi–Bini, Andresian).

- Before marking anything `ours`, search the reference works and Lewis & Short (`scripts/lookup.py`). A coinage that says what an attested word says is not entered.
- An ordinary combination of attested words (*crēta colōrāta*, *pīsa viridia*, *grūs lūsōria*) is not a coinage and not a term: put it among the record's `phrases` as a composed example.
- A coinage that is needed says why in its `comment`: what was searched and found wanting.
- Household words Jack has ruled on stay (pyjamas are *vestis nocturna*; the playground is *lūsōrium*). Never delete or replace a word a comment calls the household word or our family's own word; ask.
- The builder refuses a record with a `recommended` term of period `ours` beside a `recommended` term of another period. It does not yet require a reason on every `ours` term: the pass of 13 November 2026 covered the coinages standing beside an attested term, and Jack ruled on the doubtful ones on 14 November (`notes/coinages-2026-11-13.md`); those in records with no attested term are still to do.

See "Coinages only where needed" in `docs/records.md`.

## The answer answers the headword

Jack's rule (14 November 2026): a record's first recommended term is what the page prints first and what the practice cards show, so it must be the kind of word the headword is. *Mountain range*, a noun, had only the verb phrase *perpetuīs jugīs tendere*, and looked broken.

- A noun headword gets a noun, a verb a verb, an adjective an adjective, wherever the sources have one that truly answers it (Lewis & Short, or Traupman or Morgan–Owens by page or headword). The verbal expression stays as an alternative with its own gloss, or as an example.
- Where Latin really says the thing with a verb (*accountability* is *ratiōnem reddere*), keep the verb and say so: the gloss reads as the verbal phrase and the comment says that Latin uses a verb. Where the headword is truthfully two parts of speech, list both in `pos`.
- Never make up a noun to make the card neat.
- `python3 scripts/answer_kinds.py` lists the records to look at; read it after adding records. See "The answer answers the headword" in `docs/records.md` and `notes/answers-2026-11-14.md`.

## Practice

Jack's requirements (13 November 2026): the lexicon has a place to practise, with vocabulary as the primary thing and phrases secondary. The details are under "Practice" in README.md.

- `practice.html` (`src/practice.html`, `src/practice.js`) drills the records: an English cue, the Latin said aloud, then "Got it" or "Again", with the boxes of the drill in Jack's hub and the same record for each card, so that progress moves between the two. The reader chooses a deck by kind (words, everyday phrases, sayings and quotations), part of speech, category, source and "Mine" (the words looked up, and lists pasted by the reader). Choices within a group add up; groups narrow one another; an empty group does not narrow.
- How a card moves and what a round holds (Jack, 15 November 2026: the day after a long session every round was yesterday's words, "really boring", and a word missed once came back four times in two days). "Again" sends a card to box 0, due in 10 minutes. "Got it" sends a missed card (box 0 or 1) to box 2, due the next day; a new card to box 3; any other card up one box. Waits of a day or more are counted in calendar days, a day starting at 04:00 on the device, and from box 3 up they are spread at random (3–4, 6–8, 14–18, 30–40 days). A round of 12 has at most 6 repetitions while the deck has new cards, missed cards first and then the longest overdue, mixed among the new ones. The request is Jack's; the numbers are the coordinating session's, named constants at the top of `src/practice.js` with their grounds. Do not change the stored record (`{b, due, t}`) or the box numbers, and do not rewrite the `due` of records already stored.
- It is a game of its own (Jack, 14 November 2026). The page is the lobby (deck, meter, Start); a round is played in a layer over the whole page, with a way out (X, Escape), a card that is a real card, and real buttons at the foot of the screen. **This is a deliberate exception to "rules, not boxes or pills", for the round's layer and the Start button only, at Jack's request**: corners, a border, a shadow, a tinted ground (`--ground`, `--card`). It keeps the lexicon's faces and its ink, red and paper, in both themes; do not give it colours of its own, and do not carry its boxes into the rest of the site.
- The two marks must be told apart without reading them: *Iterum* is outlined, red, with a turning arrow, at the left; *Teneō* is filled, with a tick, at the right. A mark can be taken back once (*Redī*, `z`, Backspace): the card's record is restored exactly, a new card having none.
- The interface is in Latin by default, with English as the other choice. The Latin of the round is the hub drill's wording, which Jack knows; the strings composed for this page are marked in `src/practice.js` as Claude's, for Jack's review: do not present them as his. A new string is written in both languages, with every macron and j for consonantal i. The page's `lang` follows the choice, and every piece of text that is not in the interface's language carries its own `lang` (`la` for answers and categories, `en` for the cue, glosses, authors and citations, empty for what the reader typed): the display options change text by its nearest `lang`.
- The front of a card says what the cue leaves open (Jack, 15 November 2026: "in turn" alone could be several words): under the headword, its other English names (the record's `also`), the Latin word's gloss where it says more, and the English of an example. All of it is English; nothing on the front may give the answer away.
- The back of a card says where the answer comes from (period and first citations), gives the record's other words (the coordinating session's idea, not Jack's), and shows an example only if it contains the answer: the builder decides (`answer_test`, with the search's stems), never the page.
- The round is kept on the device, in `localStorage` (`lexicon-practice-round`, with the time it was kept), so that a reader who opens an entry from a card and comes back finds the card as it was, even in a new visit (Jack, 15 November 2026: on his iPhone a link from the entry to another site opened a sheet over the app, and after it there was no way back and no round; until then the round was in `sessionStorage`). It can be taken up for 12 hours, opens by itself for an hour, and after that is offered by the lobby (*Perge*). An entry opened from the current card shows one quiet link back to the round, drawn by `src/page.js`. Keep all of that working.
- `hora.html` (`src/hora.html`) is Jack's clock game, *Hōra cum ūnciā*. Since 15 November 2026 it wears the lexicon's colours, at Jack's request (with its own palette it "feels like something completely different"): the Minium values, under the stylesheet's names, copied into the page's own styles, the page on `--ground` and the cards on `--card` as in the practice round, and the lexicon's theme, light unless `lexicon-theme` is `dark`, never the system's. It keeps its own layout and behaviour, and its faces (the system's monospace for numbers and labels). Ink carries the cue, the hands and the main button; red the headings and labels, the control in use, the mark at twelve and a wrong answer (in dark the words cream and the marks rose, as everywhere); lapis (see Design) the twelfths, in the dial's sectors and the coin signs, and a right answer. Right and wrong differ in more than colour: a tick, a cross, and a macron where only the long vowels were missed. Do not give it green, bronze or any fourth colour; the browser tests check. It must make no request to another site and must work offline.
- The cards are `assets/practice-data.js`, generated by `practice_data` in the builder: one card per record, its kind from `practice_kind`, its sources from the table `PRACTICE_SOURCES`. A new abbreviation for an author in the data needs a line in that table, or its cards are not found under the author. The categories come from `filters` in `data/catalog.json` at build time; never list them by hand.
- Everything a reader does stays on the device, in these storage keys, each read and written inside try/catch: `lexicon-practice` (progress), `lexicon-practice-deck` (the deck's choices), `lexicon-practice-lang` (the language), `lexicon-seen` (entries opened, written by `src/page.js`, the 500 most recent), `lexicon-lists` (the reader's lists), `lexicon-review` (the reader's flags; see Review) and the clock's `hcu-…`. Do not rename them: a reader's progress would be lost. Nothing is sent to a server.
- No list tied to the unpublished readers is shipped: not in `data/`, not in the practice data, not in a page. Jack practises such word sets by pasting them as a list on his own device, which is what "My lists" is for.
- Practice is reached from the front page (a short block above the contents) and from the footer of every page. The header is fixed by design and gains no link. A concept page gains the record that it was opened and, while a round is in progress at that entry's card, the link back to the round; nothing else.
- The lobby follows the Minium design, and both the lobby and the round the phone requirements (in the round the buttons are 52px or more, and the layer respects the safe areas of an iPhone); the browser tests cover a round in its layer, the spacing (the moves, the calendar days and the 04:00 boundary with the clock set, the spread, the make-up of a round), closing and taking it up again, coming back from an entry, the back of a card, the step back, both languages, the deck, the words looked up, a pasted list, export and import (the hub's format too), the phone layout of the lobby, the round and the clock, and that the clock asks nothing of other sites.

## Review

Jack's requirement (15 November 2026): every record is a draft until he has read it. Only he reviews, and not on the site: he gives his verdicts in a private page of his own, built by `scripts/editor_review.py`. The public site keeps only the flag (Jack, 15 November 2026). The process is in `docs/review.md`; read it before touching `review` fields, `src/review.js` or `scripts/apply_review.py`.

- **Agents never set `review.status` to `reviewed`.** Only `scripts/apply_review.py` does, from a report of Jack's verdicts. An agent who changes a reviewed record sets it back to `draft` in the same commit, with the day's date, so that it is read again.
- **Jack's verdicts are in his page's database.** The page is a private claude.ai artifact titled *Recēnsiō · Lexicon Latīnitātis Cotīdiānae* (source `src/editor/`, built by `scripts/editor_review.py`). When he asks for his review to be applied: read the documents of its collection `verdicts`, save them one per line, run `python3 scripts/editor_review.py --report <file> | python3 scripts/apply_review.py -`, rebuild to see that the site still builds, and commit the records and `notes/review-flags.md`; then build the page again, publish it to the same artifact, and only then delete the applied documents. The steps are under "The editor's page" in `docs/review.md`. Read the summary: an entry "changed since you read it" was not marked, and wants telling him.
- **Publish the page again whenever the records change much** (after a batch of new records or corrections), so that he reads the text as it stands. Never make a second artifact for it, and never share it or loosen its database rule (owner only).
- A report pasted by Jack himself (a JSON block that begins `{"lexicon-review":1`) is applied the same way: `python3 scripts/apply_review.py -`. A report from anyone else, such as a GitHub issue, is applied with `--flags-only`: only the editor's own verdict marks a record reviewed.
- `notes/review-flags.md` lists what readers flagged, newest first. Act on each (check the entry against its sources; correct it or say why it stands), then delete its line. A corrected record has a new revision and is a draft to be read again.
- A verdict is tied to the record's revision (`record_revision` in the builder: a hash of the record without `review`). The page, the practice cards and `apply_review.py` must all use that one function.
- On the site: a flag (*Notā*) at the foot of every entry and on the back of every practice card, and `review.html` (footer link, never the header) with the totals and the report of the reader's flags. Flags stay on the device in `lexicon-review` until sent; do not rename the key. The flag is quiet on an entry's page (a grey sans word with an icon, red only when in use, 44px); in the game it follows the game's look.
- **Nothing of the editor's goes on the site**: no "looks right" control, no reviewer's switch, no queue of drafts. Do not add them back; the tests check that they are absent. The one trace is deliberate: `src/review.js` still reads a "looks right" verdict that the first version of 15 November 2026 may have left on a device, and puts it into the report, so that it is not lost.
- **The site never prints the reviewer's name** (Jack, 15 November 2026). A reviewed entry ends "Reviewed 15 November 2026." The record keeps `review.by`, the builder validates it, and no page, card or search datum carries it.
- The Latin of the flag and of the review page is Claude's wording, marked so in `src/review.js`, for Jack's review. It follows the practice page's language switch.

## Sources are citations

Jack's decision (13 November 2026): a line under Sources is a citation as a book prints one, not an account of how we read the book, and a work is cited once per record with all its pages. See "Sources are citations" in `docs/records.md`; the builder enforces it.

## Exported copies

Library and downloaded HTML files are snapshots. They may be stale and are not automatically synchronized with this repository. Never make a requested lexicon change only in an exported copy, and never overwrite newer repository content with an older export.

If an updated export or synchronization is requested, commit the repository changes first, then regenerate the export from that committed version. Keep a visible snapshot notice with links to the live site and repository. Compare any independently edited export with the current repository before importing changes.

## Content and verification

Preserve macrons, genitives, genders, source citations, and distinctions between attested vocabulary and working formations. Verify search and affected content after functional changes. Check deployment after publishing site changes.
