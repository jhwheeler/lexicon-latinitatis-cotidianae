# Lexicon editing instructions

## Source of truth

The authoritative lexicon is the structured data in `data/` on the `main` branch of this repository:
https://github.com/jhwheeler/lexicon-latinitatis-cotidianae

Published site:
https://jhwheeler.github.io/lexicon-latinitatis-cotidianae/

For any request to read, extend, correct, or edit the lexicon, fetch the current repository version first. New words go in as concept records in `data/concepts/` (one record per sense, with notes for every departure from Lewis & Short or a source, and rulings in `data/rulings.json`; see `docs/records.md`). Every grouped topic card in `data/entries/` was converted to records by 9 November 2026 (`scripts/convert_cards.py`, rules in `docs/converting.md`); the converted records are drafts until reviewed. Category metadata is in `data/catalog.json`. Page layout, styles, and search live in `src/`. **Commit sources only: the site is not kept in the repository** (Jack, 15 November 2026; until then about 16,000 generated files were committed with every change). `python3 scripts/build.py` validates the data and writes the site beside the sources for looking at and testing (`index.html`, `k/`, `c/`, `l/`, `words.html`, `latin.html`, the guidance and practice pages, `chartae/`, `sitemap.xml`, `manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js`, `assets/`); git ignores all of it, and a file written there by hand is overwritten. On every push to `main` the workflow `.github/workflows/pages.yml` builds the site afresh (`build.py --out _site`) and publishes that build; GitHub Pages takes the site from GitHub Actions, not from a branch. A new generated file at the top of the site needs a line in `.gitignore` (a test checks it). Run `python3 scripts/build.py`, `python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v`, `python3 scripts/check_quantities.py` (vowel lengths against Lewis & Short; a deliberate departure needs a note, a ruling, or an entry in `data/quantity-exceptions.json`), and the browser search tests (`npm test`; setup in README.md) before committing; CI runs all four. `npm test` builds the site first. After a push to `main`, see that the run of "Publish the site" succeeded: that is the deployment. See `docs/data-format.md` for the catalog and `docs/records.md` for records.

## Latin orthography: consonantal i is j

Jack's decision (29 October 2026): the lexicon follows the orthography of the Theodōrus et pater readers and writes consonantal i as j in all Latin: headwords, phrases, examples, and quotations (quotations are normalised to it).

- *jam, ejus, cujus, major, Troja, Jēsus, Jōannēs* (with long *ō* since 9 November 2026, after Lewis & Short's *Jōannes*; Jack).
- No macron on the vowel before intervocalic j (*major, ejus, pejus*: the vowel is short, the syllable heavy). Long prefixes keep theirs (*dējectus, prōjēcit, ējēcī*).
- Compounds: *abjicit, objice, dējiciō, subjēcit, conjungō, conjūnx, adjuvō*.
- i stays a vowel in the interjection *iō*, in forms of *eō* (*iērunt, adiērunt*), in *superior, interior, Plēiades*, in *Iūlus* where it scans as three syllables, after *qu/gu* (*quiēs, reliquiae*), and wherever it is a vowel (*diēs, cliēns, audiō, gaudium*).
- Only Latin changes. English, text in other languages, and link targets keep their spelling; citation labels give the dictionary's own headword (Lewis & Short prints *janua, jus* but *deicio*).

After adding or editing Latin, run `python3 scripts/consonantal_j.py --list` and review both the proposed changes and the kept candidates, then `--write` and rebuild. `--check`, also run by the tests, fails while any Latin still needs j; record a genuine exception in the script's `KEEP` list with its reason. Content ported from older branches (written with i) needs this pass. Search treats i and j alike, so *iam* finds *jam*, and u and v alike, so *uideō* finds *videō*. Consonantal u is written v in the data; the display options can show it as u (lower case only: *uideō*, but *Venus*) as they can show j as i (Jack, 11 November 2026).

## Examples from the Theodōrus et pater readers

The readers (the libellī, and the cookbook that goes with them) are Jack's own and unpublished; whether they will be published is undecided. A reader of the lexicon cannot be expected to know them as he knows Cicero, so the site never cites them (Jack, 13 November 2026):

- In the data an example taken from them keeps its place as its `source`: `Libellus III, <span lang="la">Apud avum et aviam</span>`, or `<span lang="la">Culīna Theodōrī</span>` for the cookbook. The site shows it as "composed", like our other sentences, and the search does the same. A record's source line that names the readers (it begins *Theodōrus et pater*) stays in the data and is not shown.
- Prose never leans on them: no "the word of the libellī", "as in Libellus VII", "the series spelling". Say what is true without them: "our household word", "the house spelling", "a description of ours". Do not give a libellus as a term's `evidence`.
- In the readers *fābula* means only the tales told inside them, so it is not used for the readers themselves (Jack, 30 October 2026).

Examples kept this way quote the text word for word, and the readers are still being revised, so quotations drift. Whenever the readers change, run `python3 scripts/check_libelli.py --latinitas <path to a latinitas checkout>`: it checks every such example against the current texts and each title against the reader's `@title`, and lists looser mentions to look over. Re-quote what it reports from the current text and correct the English to match. Household words follow the series bible in the latinitas repository (pyjamas are *vestis nocturna*, not Traupman's *synthesis dormītōria*).

## Languages: English and Latin

Jack's decision (8 November 2026): the lexicon is English–Latin. Don't add glosses, labels, headings, or search terms in Bulgarian or any other language by default. Cite another language only where it helps to explain a Latin word's meaning or form, as a cf. (for example, Lithuanian *ūdra* and Russian *vydra* in the ruling on *lutra*). Describe local things in English (white brined cheese, not *sirene*).

## English punctuation: the serial comma

Jack's decision (18 November 2026, on finding the lexicon written without it: "Anathema!"): our English takes the serial (Oxford) comma. In a series of three or more, a comma stands before the *and*, *or*, or *nor* that brings in the last item: "macrons, examples, and sources", "a stool, a bench, or a step", "he came in, sat down, and ate". It holds for everything we write: records (glosses, comments, grammar lines, the English of examples), rulings, the site's pages, and the repository's docs and notes.

- A pair takes no comma ("with the ablative or the dative"), and a comma that is there for another reason (an apposition, an opening phrase) does not make a pair a series.
- Only our own English changes. Words quoted from someone else keep their punctuation: a dictionary's definition in quotation marks, the title of a book, Jack's words in a note. Latin is never repunctuated to match.
- The pass of 18 November 2026 put the comma into the existing text: every place where *and* or *or* followed a comma was read and judged, and 1,123 commas went into 716 files. `notes/serial-comma-2026-11-18.md` says how. The records Jack had already reviewed stayed reviewed, by his decision for that pass (a change of punctuation only, made at his request); that is not a general exception to the rule under Review.

## Design

Jack's choice (9 November 2026): the "Minium" design. The rule is "say the black, do the red": Latin and its English are ink, and the rubrics (section heads, periods, note numbers, the control in use) are red. The text face is Gentium Book Plus, subset and renamed Lexicon Serif as its licence requires, with its own small capitals. Alegreya Sans is used only for controls. The layout uses rules, not boxes or pills (the one exception is the practice game's round: see Practice), and rules that run one above the other end at the same edges: the search has a single rule, under the field and Clear alike. Latin quoted in English prose (notes, rulings, comments) is italic.

Light, black and red on white, is the default. Jack found red on black hard to read (9 November 2026), so the dark theme is warm grey with cream ink: its headings and labels are cream, and red is kept to the small marks (note numbers, underlines, the control in use), as a soft rose. In the stylesheet, `--rubric` colours headings and labels and `--accent` the marks; in light both are red.

The lexicon has a third colour, lapis blue, the rubricator's other ink (15 November 2026): `--lapis:#24457a` with the tint `--lapis-soft:#e1e8f3` in light, `--lapis:#9db4d6` with `--lapis-soft:#2a3345` in dark; deep and a little grey in light, pale in dark, never a bright blue. It is the coordinating session's choice, made when Jack asked for a harmonious third colour for the clock, and is for Jack to judge. So far it is used only in the clock game (see Practice), and is defined only there. A future use would be right where a second kind of mark is needed and red is taken; it is not for text, headings, or decoration, and it does not replace red.

The front page has the search and the contents (Jack, 9 November 2026). The categories run from the person outward (Jack, 13 November 2026; until then they stood in the order they had been added, playground first): conversation, common words, and time; the body and clothes; the household, the house, and the kitchen; nature and food, the town, travel; play and school; work, money, tools, building, and computing; civic life and the church. The order is that of `filters` in `data/catalog.json`; a new category goes where it belongs in it, not at the end. Every page is linkable and works without JavaScript (Jack, 9 November 2026): each category has its own page (`k/<id>.html`) listing its entries from A to Z, with the categories beside it (on a phone, in a menu above), and `words.html` and `latin.html` list every headword by letter. The search, on the front page and the category pages, is the one part that needs scripts; its address keeps the query (`?q=`), and without scripts the lists stand in for it. Links to the old single page (`index.html#culina`, `#entry-528`) are redirected by `assets/legacy.js`. On a concept page the reasons (period, evidence, notes) stand in the margin beside the line they explain, and on a phone they fold under it. The header of every page is the name on the left, a search field in the middle, and one settings control, "Aa", on the right, which opens the display options (macrons shown or hidden, consonantal i as j or i, consonantal u as v or u) and the theme (Jack, 11 November 2026). The header's field is a plain form that opens the front page with `?q=`, so it sends without scripts; the front page and the category pages, which search in place, show only their own field. On a phone the field takes its own line under the name, always in view. Pressing `/` focuses the search. How we decide, Rulings, How the practice works, and About are in the footer of every page, in that order, not the header; the settings need scripts and are hidden without them. The site must work well on a phone: nothing scrolls sideways, every control is at least 44px tall, and the search field's text is at least 16px. The browser tests check all three. Opened from the home screen of an iPhone, a scrolled page showed through under the status bar (Jack, 14 November 2026): `body::before` is a strip of paper of the bar's height (`env(safe-area-inset-top)`) fixed above everything, and whatever sticks to the top of the screen sticks at that height, not at 0. This was written without a phone to try it on. The fonts and their licences are in `src/fonts/` (see its README).

## Speed, the app, and offline use

Jack's requirements (13 November 2026): the search must feel instant, with no backend, and the lexicon must open from his phone's home screen like an app, without an app store.

- The search draws 30 results at a time, with "Show 30 more" under them; the count line gives the full number. Never draw every match: a single letter matches thousands of entries, and drawing them all took seconds on a phone. The search data is fetched when the page is idle after loading, so it is ready before the first letter; while it is on its way the status line says "Loading the lexicon…" and the rule under the field moves.
- Back leads from one search to the one before it (Jack, 19 November 2026: after a second search Back took him to another website, where he expected the word he had searched for before). The search runs in place, so `src/search.js` writes the history itself: a word being typed rewrites the address in place, and a search that was looked at becomes a step of its own when the next one begins. Looked at means that the page opened with it, Enter was pressed, its results were clicked, or it stood for `LOOKED` milliseconds with something found; typing on from it (tow, towel) is the same search. The rule and the number are Claude's, for Jack to judge. Keep `popstate` drawing the search the address names. The browser tests cover it.
- `manifest.webmanifest`, the icons (`src/icons/`, drawn by `scripts/make_icons.mjs`), and the tags `app_head` puts in every page make "Add to Home Screen" open the lexicon in its own window, not a browser tab.
- The service worker (`src/sw.js`, built to `sw.js` at the root) keeps the front page, the category pages, the guidance pages, the practice pages and their cards, the stylesheet, the scripts, the search data, the fonts, and the icons on the device when it installs, and the entry and word pages as they are read (the 600 most recent). The picture cards' pictures are saved as they are read too, and never when it installs: keep them out of `assets/`, which is saved whole. They are not counted among the 600 and are never cleared away to make room (Jack, 20 November 2026: "maximī mōmentī est mē chartīs ūtī posse sine interrēte"; until then reading entries could push them out): keep `trim` in `src/sw.js` passing over `chartae/`. The lexicon then opens at once and searches offline. Its version is a hash of the files it saves, so every build that changes them makes browsers fetch them afresh: a reader sees a change at the second opening after it is published, not the first. Do not give the saved files names that change, and do not add a second cache for them.
- The browser tests cover the batches, the loading state, the manifest, and offline use.

## Words or phrases

A record is a phrase when its `pos` is `phrase` (or includes it); give every saying or sentence that part of speech. The choice is kept on the device and carries from one list to the next. ## Every Latin word linked

Jack's request (13 November 2026): every word of the lexicon's Latin leads to its entry, and every word used has one. The builder links each word of a term and of an example to the page of the word it is a form of (*potest* to *possum*, *casā* to *casa*), reading the forms from the dictionary forms in the data with their macrons; see "Every Latin word linked" in `docs/records.md`.

- Give every term its full dictionary form, correctly marked: the genitive and gender of a noun, all the principal parts of a verb. A form the dictionary form does not show is not found, and a macron that differs between an example and its entry breaks the link.
- A form that could belong to several words is linked only when the choice is safe, and otherwise left alone. Never loosen that to raise the count: a wrong link is worse than none. The rules are in `FormIndex.choose` in `scripts/build.py`; the ratio by which a commoner word may take a form (`LINK_RATIO`) and the quiet style of the links in examples (the class `tap`) are Claude's choices, for Jack to judge.
- A saying is its own headword, commas and all (Jack, 18 November 2026: the page for *Eho* was the saying *Eho, nōn tam cito!*, defined as "whoa, not so fast!", with an example of *ohē*). `term_head` cuts a dictionary form at its first comma (*Rōma, Rōmae f.*) and a saying only at a bracket (*Grātulor (tibi)!*), so a sentence never becomes a page for its first word; it is listed on the pages of its words instead, the one-word sayings among them (*Eho tū!* under *Eho!*). And a word's page shows an example only if the example has the word in it, in any of its forms (`answer_test`, as on the practice cards): an entry's examples may be of its other words.
- The same day's sweep for bugs of that kind (Claude's, at Jack's request, for him to judge). A saying is not cut at a bracket inside it either (*Nōlī (nōlīte) exscrībere ex proximō.* had become the word *Nōlī*), only at one that ends it (*Grātulor (tibi)!*). **A phrase with a comma that is not a dictionary form must be written as a saying, with a capital and a final stop**, or it is cut at the comma: *modo sīc, modo sīc* gave the practice card the answer *modo sīc*, and *ūsus, magister ēgregius* put "experience is the best teacher" among the senses of *ūsus*. A one-word saying keeps its own page (*Sānē.* beside *sānē*, *Ecce!* beside *Ecce.*), and each such page lists the others of the same word. Words that share a head are set out apart at the top of the page, each with its dictionary form and its senses (*volō, velle* and *volō, volāre*; *frōns, frondis* and *frōns, frontis*; the noun *medicus* and the adjective): `word_homographs`. Two dictionary forms are one word when they are of the same kind and their second form is the same, so give a word the same genitive, gender, and "pl." in every record (*mēlopepō, mēlopeponis*, as the note under melon rules).
- After adding records, run `python3 scripts/latin_unlinked.py` and read `notes/coverage/unlinked.md`: the words without an entry are the list for new records, and the words "found only when the macrons are ignored" are usually slips to correct.

## Word families

Jack's request (13 November 2026, and 15 November: "let's try and do all those things"): the page for *acrobat* gave *petaurista* and did not lead to *petaurum*, under *trampoline*. The builder now works out each word's family, the other headwords made from the same root, and shows it as a line "Same family:" on the word's page and under the answer on a concept's page; see "Word families" in `docs/records.md`.

- **A wrong family is worse than none.** Stems are compared with their vowel lengths, the suffixes and prefixes are explicit lists in `scripts/build.py`, a word that could belong to several families is joined to none, and the root must have three letters. Never loosen this to raise the count.
- `data/families.json` is small and kept by hand: `apart` for look-alikes the method cannot tell apart (*calamitās, calamus*), `together` for families it cannot see (*absēns* with *absum*), `homographs` for two words with the same forms under one head (*jūs*). Every entry carries a note of its authority, read that day in Lewis & Short or de Vaan (`scripts/lookup.py`); nothing goes in from memory.
- After adding many records, run `python3 scripts/family_sample.py --list` and read what is new; a look-alike the method joined goes into `apart`. A word renamed or removed from the records must be taken out of the file too: the builder says which.
- A term's dictionary form decides its family, as it decides its links: a macron that differs between two records of one word (*lustrō* beside *lūstrō*) splits it or joins it to a stranger.
- The method, its lists, the minimum of three letters, the eight words shown on a concept's page, and the wording of the label are Claude's choices, for Jack to judge. The checks are written up in `notes/families-2026-11-15.md`: every proposed family was read once, and two seeded samples were checked pair by pair against Lewis & Short. The second, of 100 families, had one wrong family.
- Nearby concepts (acrobat, juggler, circus) are another thing: "See also" lists them by hand, and proposals by embeddings are still to do (README, "To do").
- **New words make new families, and nobody has read them.** After adding records, list the families before and after (`python3 scripts/family_sample.py --list`), read every one that is new or changed, and put the wrong joins in `data/families.json` with the authority. On 15 November 2026 the 179 words of the third frequency batch made 102 new or changed families, of which four were wrong (*labium* with *labō*, *hostia* with *hostis*, *mūnia* with *mūniō*, the verb *sector* with *secō*).

## Coinages only where needed

Jack's principle (13 November 2026): a word or phrase we came up with (a term whose `period` is `ours`) belongs in the lexicon only when it is needed: when no author of any period has a word for the concept, or a circumlocution has a really good reason. Otherwise take the word from earlier authors; they need not be classical, though the closer to Cicero the more weight. The modern reference works count as sources (Traupman's *Conversational Latin*, Morgan–Owens, Levi–Bini, Andresian).

- Before marking anything `ours`, search the reference works and Lewis & Short (`scripts/lookup.py`). A coinage that says what an attested word says is not entered.
- An ordinary combination of attested words (*crēta colōrāta*, *pīsa viridia*, *grūs lūsōria*) is not a coinage and not a term: put it among the record's `phrases` as a composed example.
- A coinage that is needed says why in its `comment`: what was searched and found wanting.
- Household words Jack has ruled on stay (pyjamas are *vestis nocturna*; the playground is *lūsōrium*). Never delete or replace a word a comment calls the household word or our family's own word; ask.
- The builder refuses a record with a `recommended` term of period `ours` beside a `recommended` term of another period. It does not yet require a reason on every `ours` term: the pass of 13 November 2026 covered the coinages standing beside an attested term, and Jack ruled on the doubtful ones on 14 November (`notes/coinages-2026-11-13.md`); those in records with no attested term (219 records) were checked on 15 November 2026 (`notes/coinages-2026-11-15-a.md`, `-b.md`): where nothing attested answers the concept, an ordinary description stays as the record's term, still marked ours, and its comment says that it is a description and what was searched. Jack ruled on the doubtful ones on 16 November 2026; each ruling stands in those notes under its case, with what was done. A classical word used of a modern thing is labelled `classical`, with the new sense in the gloss and a comment that the use is modern, and ours (Jack, 16 November 2026, for *aestimātor*, *statiō*, *sellula*, and *cubus*, after *mendum*).

See "Coinages only where needed" in `docs/records.md`.

## The answer answers the headword

Jack's rule (14 November 2026): a record's first recommended term is what the page prints first and what the practice cards show, so it must be the kind of word the headword is. *Mountain range*, a noun, had only the verb phrase *perpetuīs jugīs tendere*, and looked broken.

- A noun headword gets a noun, a verb a verb, an adjective an adjective, wherever the sources have one that truly answers it (Lewis & Short, or Traupman or Morgan–Owens by page or headword). The verbal expression stays as an alternative with its own gloss, or as an example.
- Where Latin really says the thing with a verb (*accountability* is *ratiōnem reddere*), keep the verb and say so: the gloss reads as the verbal phrase and the comment says that Latin uses a verb. Where the headword is truthfully two parts of speech, list both in `pos`.
- Never make up a noun to make the card neat.
- `python3 scripts/answer_kinds.py` lists the records to look at; read it after adding records. See "The answer answers the headword" in `docs/records.md` and `notes/answers-2026-11-14.md`.

## Practice

Jack's requirements (13 November 2026): the lexicon has a place to practise, with vocabulary as the primary thing and phrases secondary. The details are under "Practice" in README.md.

- `practice.html` (`src/practice.html`, `src/practice.js`) drills the records: an English cue, the Latin said aloud, then "Got it" or "Again", with the boxes of the drill in Jack's hub and the same record for each card, so that progress moves between the two. The reader chooses a deck by kind (words, everyday phrases, sayings, and quotations), part of speech, category, source, and "Mine" (the words looked up, and lists pasted by the reader). Choices within a group add up; groups narrow one another; an empty group does not narrow.
- How a card moves and what a round holds (Jack, 15 November 2026: the day after a long session every round was yesterday's words, "really boring", and a word missed once came back four times in two days). "Again" sends a card to box 0, due in 10 minutes. "Got it" sends a missed card (box 0 or 1) to box 2, due the next day; a new card to box 3; any other card up one box. Waits of a day or more are counted in calendar days, a day starting at 04:00 on the device, and from box 3 up they are spread at random (3–4, 6–8, 14–18, 30–40 days). A round of 12 has at most 6 repetitions while the deck has new cards, missed cards first and then the longest overdue, mixed among the new ones. The request is Jack's; the numbers are the coordinating session's, named constants at the top of `src/practice.js` with their grounds. Do not change the stored record (`{b, due, t}`) or the box numbers, and do not rewrite the `due` of records already stored. Since the same day the boxes go on above the hub's six to about 80 days, half a year, and a year (`TOP` is 9), and repetition takes more of a round as cards wait (`ROUND_DUE_MORE`: 8 of 12 with more than 36 due, 10 with more than 72): both came out of writing the page "How the practice works" (`src/how-practice-works.html`), which gives the evidence for each rule and says which numbers are our own choice. Change a rule in the script and on that page together; a test compares the two.
- `how-practice-works.html` (`src/how-practice-works.html`) says how the practice chooses and spaces its cards, on what evidence, and which numbers are our own choice (Jack, 15 November 2026: the spacing research and its sources on a page of the site, "similar to how we rule about words"). It is a guidance page like How we decide, in English, with no script of its own, linked from every footer and from the lobby under the note that progress is kept on the device. When a constant or a rule of `src/practice.js` changes, change the page in the same commit; a test compares its numbers with the constants. A claim about research goes on it only with a source that was read, linked in the text and listed under Sources as a book prints a citation; what a study was done on (prose passages, Swahili pairs, undergraduates) is said; nothing is cited from memory, and a source that could not be read is left out. `notes/practice-spacing-2026-11-15.md` records what was read and what each source supports: add to it when a source is added. The analysis on the page (what supports each rule, and where the evidence leans against one) is Claude's, for Jack to judge.
- It is a game of its own (Jack, 14 November 2026). The page is the lobby (deck, meter, Start); a round is played in a layer over the whole page, with a way out (X, Escape), a card that is a real card, and real buttons at the foot of the screen. **This is a deliberate exception to "rules, not boxes or pills", for the round's layer and the Start button only, at Jack's request**: corners, a border, a shadow, a tinted ground (`--ground`, `--card`). It keeps the lexicon's faces and its ink, red, and paper, in both themes; do not give it colours of its own, and do not carry its boxes into the rest of the site.
- The two marks must be told apart without reading them: *Iterum* is outlined, red, with a turning arrow, at the left; *Teneō* is filled, with a tick, at the right. A mark can be taken back once (*Redī*, `z`, Backspace): the card's record is restored exactly, a new card having none.
- The interface is in Latin by default, with English as the other choice. The Latin of the round is the hub drill's wording, which Jack knows; the strings composed for this page are marked in `src/practice.js` as Claude's, for Jack's review: do not present them as his. What he has approved is named in the comments with the date: on 16 November 2026 *Quā ratiōne repetātur* (the lobby's link to How the practice works) and *Redī ad cursum* (`src/page.js`, the link from an entry back to the round), and on his own page (`src/editor/review.js`) *Retrō*, *Tolle*, *Quaere verbum*, *Rērum genera*, *In lexicō aperī*, *Jūdicia servantur*, *Nōndum servātum*, and *Iterum temptā*. An approval covers the strings he was asked about and no others. A new string is written in both languages, with every macron and j for consonantal i. The page's `lang` follows the choice, and every piece of text that is not in the interface's language carries its own `lang` (`la` for answers and categories, `en` for the cue, glosses, authors, and citations, empty for what the reader typed): the display options change text by its nearest `lang`.

## Sources are citations

Jack's decision (13 November 2026): a line under Sources is a citation as a book prints one, not an account of how we read the book, and a work is cited once per record with all its pages. See "Sources are citations" in `docs/records.md`; the builder enforces it.

The notes that hold for every entry are said once (Jack, 15 November 2026), in the section "How to read the sources" of `src/how-we-decide.html`, to which every entry with sources links. Never write such a sentence in a record's `sources`: the builder refuses it and says what the line should be. `python3 scripts/standing_notes.py --write` takes the sentences out of records that still carry them (a branch drafted before the rule, a converted batch); `notes/standing-notes-2026-11-15.md` lists what was taken out and what was left.

Jack's rulings of 16 November 2026 on what was left:

- **The section on the site is cut to the essentials**: four points and under 150 words (what "Lewis & Short" after a quotation means, what "composed" means, how the reference works are cited, where the other quotations were checked). A test holds it to that. The fuller account (which texts and editions, on which days, the Vulgate, Publilius, Graesse's, and Morgan–Owens's digitisations) is in `docs/records.md`, "How the sources were read": a new note of the kind goes there, and a remark true of one record only goes into a comment, a mark on the example, or a sentence of its own.
- **The mark belongs on the quotation.** No line under Sources says "Cicero as Lewis & Short quotes him": the quotation itself carries "(Lewis & Short)", at the end of the example's `source` or of the term's `evidence` line. The builder refuses the line and says so; `scripts/standing_notes.py` lists it and fails.
- **A public entry does not cite a working note.** No line of `sources` links to a file of this repository or of the latinitas repository, or names one. Cite the published source the note names, after reading it, or nothing. The builder refuses the line.

## Exported copies

Library and downloaded HTML files are snapshots. They may be stale and are not automatically synchronized with this repository. Never make a requested lexicon change only in an exported copy, and never overwrite newer repository content with an older export.

If an updated export or synchronization is requested, commit the repository changes first, then regenerate the export from that committed version. Keep a visible snapshot notice with links to the live site and repository. Compare any independently edited export with the current repository before importing changes.
