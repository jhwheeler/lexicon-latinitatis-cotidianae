# Lexicon Latīnitātis Cotīdiānae

Everyday English–Latin vocabulary, with macrons, examples, usage notes, and sources.

Site: https://jhwheeler.github.io/lexicon-latinitatis-cotidianae/

## Editing

**This repository is the authoritative source.** Fetch current `main` before editing.

- `data/catalog.json`: ordered categories, their headings, and filter order.
- `data/concepts/<concept>.json`: one record per concept, with notes (footnotes) on every departure from a dictionary or source; each gets its own page in `c/`. See [docs/records.md](docs/records.md).
- `data/rulings.json`: editorial rulings, listed on `rulings.html`; `src/how-we-decide.html` holds the editorial policy.
- `data/families.json`: what the builder's word families need by hand: look-alikes never to join, families the forms do not show, homographs. See "Word families" in [docs/records.md](docs/records.md).
- `data/entries/<category>.json`: the grouped topic cards of the first edition. Every card was converted to records by 9 November 2026, so the files are empty; the builder still reads the format.
- `src/page.html`: the front page and its editorial review notes; `src/how-we-decide.html`: the policy page; `src/how-practice-works.html`: how the practice chooses and spaces its cards, with the research and its sources (see Practice below).
- `src/styles.css`: styling; `src/page.js`: the theme and display options and the example filters, on every page.
- `src/search.js`: the search, on the front page and the category pages.
- `src/practice.html`, `src/practice.js`: the vocabulary practice; `src/hora.html`: the clock game; `src/chartae.html`, `src/chartae.js`, `data/chartae.json`, `src/chartae/`: the picture cards of animals, plants, and the body (*Chartae pictae*), their data, and their pictures, with `scripts/fetch_picture.py` to fetch and crop a picture for a card; `src/lacunae.html`, `src/lacunae/`: the gap games' engine and one data file per game, *Ubi est Theodōrus?*, *Quis pulsat?*, *Hic an ille?*, *Bis bīna*, and *Heri, hodiē, crās* (see Practice below).
- `src/review.html`, `src/review.js`: the flag on every entry and the page that gathers a reader's flags into a report; `scripts/apply_review.py` applies a review report to the records. The editor does not review on the site: he has a private page of his own, built by `scripts/editor_review.py` (see Review below and [docs/review.md](docs/review.md)).
- `src/editor/`, `scripts/editor_review.py`: the editor's private review page, published as an artifact and not part of the site; the script builds it into `.editor-review/` (not committed) and turns its saved verdicts into a report.
- `scripts/build.py`: validates the data and generates the site: the front page, a page per category, the English and Latin words from A to Z, a page per record and per Latin word, the rulings, the search data, the practice pages with their cards, and the redirects from the old single page.
- `scripts/consonantal_j.py`: writes consonantal i as j in the Latin (the lexicon's orthography; see AGENTS.md) and checks that none is left.
- `scripts/convert_cards.py`, `scripts/check_quantities.py`, `scripts/check_libelli.py`, `scripts/coverage.py`, `scripts/lookup.py`: converting cards, checking vowel lengths against Lewis & Short and quotations against the readers, measuring coverage of core word lists, and looking words up in the reference works (see AGENTS.md and `docs/`).
- `scripts/family_sample.py`: draws a seeded sample of the word families with Lewis & Short's derivations, for checking them; also lists every family and the words left out as ambiguous.
- `scripts/latin_frequency.py`, `scripts/latin_coverage.py`: the frequency list of classical Latin lemmas (`refs/latin-frequency.tsv`, from the LASLA corpus, which is kept outside the repository), the lexicon's coverage of it (`notes/coverage/latin-frequency.md`), and word lists work by work (`notes/coverage/works.md`). See `refs/README.md` for the source and its licence.

Edit the topic JSON, then run these commands from the repository root (Python 3.10 or newer; no packages to install):

```sh
python3 scripts/build.py
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v
```

Commit the source changes only. **The site is not kept in the repository** (Jack, 15 November 2026; until then every change carried thousands of generated files). The build writes it beside the sources, for looking at and for the tests (`index.html`, `k/`, `c/`, `l/`, `words.html`, `latin.html`, `how-we-decide.html`, `how-practice-works.html`, `rulings.html`, `practice.html`, `hora.html`, `chartae.html` with the pictures in `chartae/`, `theodorus.html`, `quis-pulsat.html`, `hic-an-ille.html`, `bis-bina.html`, `heri-hodie-cras.html`, `review.html`, `sitemap.xml`, `manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js`, and `assets/` with the stylesheet, the scripts, the search data, the fonts, and the icons), and git ignores all of it. Do not edit generated files: the next build overwrites them. The PR check rejects invalid data or a broken link between pages. The counts, the lists, and the contents are generated.

**Publishing.** On every push to `main`, `.github/workflows/pages.yml` builds the site (`python3 scripts/build.py --out _site`: the site alone, in a folder of its own) and publishes that build; GitHub Pages is set to take the site from GitHub Actions (Settings, Pages, Source). The run named "Publish the site" is the deployment: if it fails, the site keeps its last state.

See [the data format and architecture decision](docs/data-format.md) for an entry example, field definitions, and why generation happens before publication rather than in the browser.

## Pages and search

The site is plain pages, linkable and readable without JavaScript (Jack, 9 November 2026):

- `index.html`, the front page: the search, a line on what the lexicon is, and the contents, the categories with their entry counts.
- `k/<category>.html`: a category's entries from A to Z, each with its Latin, linked to its page; the categories stand beside the list (on a phone, in a menu above it). A record can be listed under more categories than its own (`also_under`).
- `words.html` and `latin.html`: every English and every Latin headword from A to Z, by letter.
- `c/<concept>.html`: a record's page, and `l/<word>.html`: a Latin word's page, which lists every entry that uses it (see [docs/records.md](docs/records.md)). Both give the word's family, the other headwords made from the same root, as a line "Same family". Every word of a term and of an example leads to the page of the word it is a form of: underlined in a term, and in an example in plain ink, underlined only under the pointer. On a concept page the reasons stand in the margin beside the line they explain and fold under it on a phone; a filter shows all examples, only the quotations, or only the composed sentences.

The search runs on the front page, over every category, and on each category's page, over that category. Its data (`assets/search-data.js`) is fetched once the page has loaded and is idle, as a script, so it is ready before the first letter is typed and also works from pages opened on disk; while it is on its way the status line says so and the rule under the field moves. The results are drawn 30 at a time, with a button for more: a single letter matches thousands of entries, and drawing them all made typing slow. The address keeps the query (`index.html?q=pyjama`, or `&cat=vestitus` for one category), so a search can be bookmarked or sent. A search that was looked at (opened with the page, sent with Enter, clicked on, or left standing for a moment with something found) is a step of its own in the browser's history once the next search begins, so Back leads from one search to the one before it; a word still being typed rewrites the address in place. A Latin word typed whole (*mons*) finds first the entry whose answer it is, then the entries that have it among their other words; a term that has the query in its own Latin or gloss ranks before examples, and those before a term that has it only in its grammar or evidence (labelled "Evidence or grammar") and before notes. Matches are highlighted with the same macron-insensitive, i/j-, and u/v-insensitive folding the search uses; each result links to its entry's page. Matches found only in other search terms are identified. A Latin word is found from any of its forms: the build gives each term the stems its forms are built on (from the genitive, or the principal parts), with the endings each takes, and the search takes an ending off what was typed, so *piscīnam* finds *piscīna* and *cucurrit* finds *currō*. When nothing matches, the entries one letter off (two in a long word) are offered as close spellings. Without scripts the search bar is hidden and the contents, the category lists, and the A–Z pages stand in for it.

The category lists and both A–Z indexes have a filter, All / Words / Phrases (a record whose part of speech is `phrase` is a phrase); the choice is kept on the device. The Latin index lists each Latin word, with its page, and the Latin of each phrase, leading to its entry; both indexes are ordered by letters alone, so *-ne* stands under N. The front page links to the two indexes above the categories. Examples taken from the editor's own unpublished readers are shown as "composed" (see AGENTS.md).

Links to the old single page still work: `index.html#culina` leads to `k/culina.html`, `#entry-528` to the record that replaced that card, `#c-socks` to `c/socks.html`. The table is `assets/legacy.js`, which the front page loads only when an address carries such a fragment.

Practice is linked from the front page, above the contents, and from every page's footer (see Practice below). The guidance pages, `how-we-decide.html`, `rulings.html`, and `how-practice-works.html`, are linked from every footer, in that order, before About.

Every page's header has the name, a search field, and one settings control ("Aa"). The field opens the front page with the query (`index.html?q=…`), as a plain form; the front page and the category pages show only their own search, which runs in place. On a phone the field has its own line under the name. `/` focuses the search. How we decide, Rulings, and About are linked from every page's footer. The settings hold the theme and the display options: light mode is the default, dark mode can be chosen and is saved locally; the display options hide the macrons, write consonantal i as i, or write consonantal u as u (lower case only, so *Venus* keeps its V), for readers of other traditions; it changes only what is shown, on every page, and the choice is kept the same way. Saved preferences are applied before the page is drawn, and the toggle still works when browser storage is unavailable. The design ("Minium") and its phone requirements are in AGENTS.md; the fonts and their licences are in `src/fonts/`.

Browser regression checks use Node.js and Playwright (development only; the published page has no runtime dependencies):

```sh

```

`npm test` runs two files: `tests/search.browser.test.mjs`, the site, and `tests/editor.browser.test.mjs`, the editor's private review page, which it builds into a temporary folder and serves with a stand-in for the artifact's database.

The checks cover the front page and its contents, the category pages and the A–Z pages, the pyjama lookup, highlighting and Unicode normalization, inflected forms and close spellings, the search within a category and linkable searches, alias and source matches, literal input, the old single page's links, every page without scripts, the header's search and the `/` key, the footer's links, the theme and display options, the phone layout of every kind of page, and practice: a round in its layer, the spacing of the repetitions and what a round holds, the back of a card, the step back, Latin and English, the deck's choices, the words looked up, a pasted list, export and import, the phone layout of the lobby and of the round, the clock game (its colours and its faces, and that it asks nothing of other sites), the picture cards (the picture and the back of a card, an animal's and a plant's, the scientific name after the English one and untouched by the display options, a second Latin word under the first, the spacing with the clock set, the three subjects and the kinds, the deck kept by the first version, both languages, the phone layout, and the pictures offline), the gap games (how they judge and show their gaps, *j* read as *i*, a round by its hash, each game's own keys, both languages, their phone layout, and that they ask nothing of other sites), and the review: flagging an entry, that the site offers the flag and none of the editor's controls, the report and Sent, a flag from a practice card, both languages. ## Practice

Four pages for practising: the vocabulary, the clock, the picture cards, and *Ubi est Theodōrus?*. They are linked from the front page, and Practice from every footer (Jack, 13 November 2026). The header gains no link.

**Vocabulary** (`practice.html`, from `src/practice.html` and `src/practice.js`). An English cue; say the Latin aloud; show it; mark "Got it" or "Again". Spaced repetition in the seven boxes of the drill in the editor's own hub (10 minutes, 1 hour, 1, 3, 7, 16, and 35 days), kept in the same form, so that progress moves between the two. Since 15 November 2026 the cards move through them differently (Jack: the day after a long session every round was that session's words again, and a word missed once came back four times in two days):

- **"Again"** sends any card back to the first box, to be seen again after 10 minutes. It is not put back into the same round.
- **"Got it"** on a card that was missed goes straight to the 1-day box, without the hour's step; on a new card, known at first sight, to the 3-day box; on any other card, up one box. Above the hub's seven boxes there are three more since 15 November 2026 (about 80 days, half a year, a year), so the waits go on growing to a year and stop there. So a word that is missed is shown once more the same day, then the next day, then three days on; a word known at first sight is next shown three or four days later.
- **A wait of a day or more is counted in days of the calendar**, and a day starts at 04:00 on the device: a card marked at 20:00 for the next day is due from 04:00, not from 20:00, and a session after midnight belongs to the evening before.
- **The longer waits are spread**, so that words learned together do not all come back together: 3 or 4 days, 6 to 8, 14 to 18, 30 to 40, then 70 to 90, 150 to 210, and 300 to 420, a whole number of days chosen at random. The 1-day wait is always the next day.
- **A round has 12 cards**, one for each Latin headword. While the deck still has new cards, at most 6 of them are repetitions: first the cards that were missed and are waiting, then the longest overdue, and among cards due on the same day in no order. New cards, in no order, are the rest, and the round is mixed. With fewer than 6 due, new cards fill the round; with no new cards left, due ones do. Half and half cannot last for a reader who takes new cards daily (more would come due than the rounds hold), so the share grows as cards wait: with more than 36 due, 8 of the 12 are repetitions, and with more than 72, 10. The reasons and the sources are on the site's page "How the practice works" (`src/how-practice-works.html`); keep it in step with the script.

The numbers are constants at the top of `src/practice.js` and are the coordinating session's choice, not the editor's. They follow Anki's defaults (a learned card graduates to 1 day, an easy one to 4; a forgotten one waits 10 minutes and then a day) and two findings: that a first retrieval after a real delay matters more than whether the later gaps grow (Karpicke & Roediger 2007, *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition* 33, 704–719), and that growing and equal gaps left much the same recall in the end (Kang, Lindsey, Mozer & Pashler 2014, *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* 21, 1544–1550). Records already kept on a device keep the day they were due.

**How the practice works** (`how-practice-works.html`, from `src/how-practice-works.html`; Jack, 15 November 2026: the research on spacing, with its sources, on a page of the site, "similar to how we rule about words"). A guidance page beside How we decide: what a round is, when a word comes back, the evidence with each claim's source, which numbers are our own choice, and what the practice does not do. It is linked from every footer and from the lobby, under the note that progress is kept on the device (*Quā ratiōne repetātur* / "How the practice works"; the Latin is Claude's, approved by the editor on 16 November 2026). It states the constants of `src/practice.js`: change them and the page together (a test compares the two). Every source in its list was read, and `notes/practice-spacing-2026-11-15.md` records for each the address read, whether it was the full text, and what in it supports the page; a claim goes on the page only with a source that was read, and a source that could not be read is not cited.

Since 14 November 2026 it is a game of its own (Jack: "it doesn't feel like a standalone game… you could X out of it… actual cards, rounded cornered cards"):

- **The page is the lobby**: the deck with its counts, a meter (known, learning, new), a Start button, the language, the backup, and the link to the clock.
- **A round is played in a layer over the whole page** (a `<dialog>`; no header, search, or footer shows). Its top bar has the way out (X, or Escape), the deck's name, a step back, and the position ("4 / 12") over a line that fills as the round goes. The card is a real card on a tinted ground; a tap on it shows the answer. The buttons are at the foot of the screen: *Respōnsum ostende*, then *Iterum* (outlined, red, a turning arrow) and *Teneō* (filled, a tick), so that they can be told apart without reading them. Keys: Space or Enter shows, 1 is Again, 2 is Got it, z or Backspace steps back.
- **The front of a card** has the English headword and, under it, what the headword leaves open: its other English names, the Latin word's gloss where it says more, and the English of an example (Jack, 15 November 2026). The names are English, so their label is English in both interfaces, written as an entry's page writes it: "also take turns, in turns, alternately" (Jack, 16 November 2026: in the Latin interface the line began "Item:", which read as the English word "item").
- **The back of a card** gives the Latin; under it where the answer comes from (its period and its first one or two citations: "classical · Cic. Tusc. 5.97"; a word of our own says "our formation", *locūtiō nostra*, beside its asterisk); the record's other words that are not to be avoided (*Item:* … in Latin, "Also: …" in English, up to three); the gloss; one example; and a link to the entry.
- **An example is shown only if it contains the answer.** A record's examples illustrate all its terms, and a card used to show a sentence without its word. The builder keeps an example only when one of its words is a form of the answer's head, by the stems the search is built on (`answer_test`); for a term of several words each of them must be there, in any order and any form. No such example, none is shown.
- **One step back** (*Redī*): the card just marked comes back with its answer shown and its record exactly as it was before the mark (a new card has none again), to be marked again. It is offered until the next mark, also from the end of the round.
- **The end of a round** gives the tally, what is waiting or when the next card comes back, and the words that were marked Again.
- **The round is kept on the device** (`localStorage`, with its time): a reader who opens a card's entry and comes back finds the same card with its answer shown, also in a new visit, as happens on an iPhone after a link from the entry to another site (Jack, 15 November 2026). A round can be taken up for 12 hours; it opens by itself for an hour, and after that the lobby offers it. The entry opened from the current card has a link back to the round. A round left in the middle and closed can be taken up from the lobby; a finished one is over.
- **In Latin by default**, with a switch *Latīnē / English* (kept on the device). The Latin of the round is the hub drill's own; the rest was composed for the page and is marked in `src/practice.js` as awaiting the editor's review, except what he has approved, which the comments there and in `src/page.js` name with the date (*Quā ratiōne repetātur* and *Redī ad cursum*, 16 November 2026). The page's `lang` follows the choice; the English cue, the glosses, and the authors' names are marked English, and the Latin Latin, so the display options (macrons, j, v) change the Latin only.

Vocabulary is the primary thing and phrases are secondary, so the deck starts as the words. The reader narrows it under "Deck", by groups of choices:

- **Kind**: words, everyday phrases, sayings, and quotations. A record is a word unless its part of speech is `phrase`. A phrase is a saying when its main term is in Traupman's list of proverbs and sayings (pp. 254–259), or is a line of Publilius Syrus, or is cited only from authors and is a whole statement (a sentence of three words or more ending in a full stop or a question mark, whose English headword is not a turn of conversation: three words or more, no "I", "me", or "you", no contraction, no exclamation mark). Every other phrase is an everyday phrase. The rule is `practice_kind` in the builder.
- **Part of speech**: nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, other. A choice among the words; a record that is two things counts under each.
- **Category**: the lexicon's categories, in the order of `filters` in `data/catalog.json`.
- **Source**: the author or handbook that a citation opens with (`Cic. Att. 5.2.1`, `Traupman 73`), read from the main term's evidence and from the examples' sources, and under an author the work where it can be told apart (`Verg. G.`). "(Lewis & Short)" at the end of a citation says where it was taken from, not who wrote it. The table of abbreviations is `PRACTICE_SOURCES` in the builder; a source is offered once it has 15 cards.
- **Mine**: "Words I looked up" (every entry page opened on this device) and the reader's own lists. A list is a name and pasted items, one per line or separated by commas; an item finds a record by its id (`brush-teeth`), its English headword (with or without the sense in brackets), or the Latin headword of its main term (macrons optional).

Within a group the choices add up (Cicero or Vergil); between groups they narrow (Cicero, within the kitchen); a group with nothing chosen does not narrow. The deck's line always gives the count: cards, due now, new.

The cards are `assets/practice-data.js` (`window.lexiconPractice`, a script so that the page works from disk), built from the records: one card per record, with short keys documented at `practice_data` in the builder. Examples taken from the editor's unpublished readers are sentences of ours there too, with no label.

**The clock** (`hora.html`, from `src/hora.html`): *Hōra cum ūnciā*, the editor's game for the twelfths of the Roman *as* and for telling the time with them, with a Latin and an English interface. Since 15 November 2026 it wears the lexicon's colours (ink, red, and paper on the practice round's tinted ground, in both themes), with lapis blue as a third colour for the twelfths and for a right answer; its layout and behaviour are its own. Since 16 November 2026 its faces are the lexicon's as well (Jack: the tally, the chip, and the headings were still in the system's monospace; "set them in the lexicon's faces"): Alegreya Sans for controls and counters, Lexicon Serif in small capitals for headings and table heads, the serif for the Latin and for the time asked, and nothing in a monospace. **The picture cards** (`chartae.html`, from `src/chartae.html`, `src/chartae.js`, and `data/chartae.json`; Jack, 20 November 2026): *Chartae pictae* ("Picture cards" in English), made for the editor and his small son. They began the same day as the animal cards, *Chartae animālium*; then the editor decided that cards on other subjects (plants and their parts first, later everyday tools and actions) go into this same game: one exercise in which the subjects are chosen, not separate games. The third subject, the same day, is the human body (Jack: every part people talk about, outside and inside, because his small son asks about them daily). The cue is a picture; the reader says the Latin name aloud and turns the card, which keeps its height as it turns (Jack, 20 November 2026): the back is in its place from the start, unseen. The back gives the dictionary form, a second Latin word for the same thing where there is one, the English name, a sentence with the word in it (a line of an author with its place, or one of ours), what an animal eats (*Quō vēscitur?* and the answer in the ablative) or what a plant bears (*Quid fert?* and the answer in the accusative), a link to the entry, and the photograph's credit.

- **Subjects and kinds.** `data/chartae.json` has three lists: `subjects` (*Animālia*, *Plantae*, *Corpus*), `groups` (the kinds, each with its `subject`: for the animals *Vīlla*, *Silva et ager*, *Avēs*, *Bēstiolae*, *Mare*, *Longinqua*; for the plants *Arborēs*, *Fruticēs et herbae*, *Pōmārium et vīnea*, *Hortus*, *Segetēs*, *Partēs plantārum*; for the body *Caput et faciēs*, *Truncus*, *Bracchium et manus*, *Crūs et pēs*, *Intus*), and `cards`. A kind with no cards yet is allowed and is not shown. An animal's card must have `food`; a plant's card may have `bears`; neither is allowed on a card of any other subject, so a card of the body has neither. A card's id is unique across every subject: it is the picture's name and the key of its progress. A kind may share its id with a card (the kind *caput* and the card *caput*, the body's kind *truncus* and the plant card *truncus*): kinds and cards are two sets of names, never looked up in each other, and the deck kept on the device holds kinds while the progress holds cards. The names of the body's subject and kinds are the coordinating session's wording, for the editor to judge.
- **A second Latin word** (Jack, 20 November 2026: where two Latin words mean the same thing, as *coma* and *capillus*, one card with the second word on its back). Any card may have `also`, a second dictionary form ("coma, comae f."), right after `latin`; the back shows it directly under the first, quietly, after the label the vocabulary practice uses for its line of other Latin words (*Item:* in Latin, "Also:" in English), and the word is marked Latin, so that the display options reach it. A card without it has no such line.
- **Scientific names** (Jack, 20 November 2026: "Hōc modō discēmus discrīmina", so as to learn where the scientific name agrees with the Latin word and where it differs: *merula* is *Turdus merula*; *īlex* is *Quercus ilex*, but *aquifolium* is *Ilex aquifolium*). Every card of an animal or a plant whose word names a taxon has `scientific`, right after `english`, shown on the back after the English name: "Anglicē oak · *Quercus*". It is written as nomenclature writes it (genus capitalised, epithets lower case, no macrons, no author), and the display options leave it as it is: it is marked `lang="la-x-taxon"`, which they do not rewrite. The name is the taxon the Latin word names, as nearly as science has one: a species where the word means one species or the one ordinarily meant in Europe and pictured, a genus, family, or order where the word is wider, a domestic animal's binomial in current use, and none for a word that is not a taxon (*glāns*). That rule is the coordinating session's, for the editor to judge; the full statement is in AGENTS.md. On a card of the body (Jack, 20 November 2026: on these cards the scientific names matter most) `scientific` is the term of the *Terminologia Anatomica*, the anatomists' international Latin, written as they write it: the first word capitalised, no macrons (*Hepar*, *Vesica urinaria*, *Encephalon*). It shows where the anatomists' word differs from the everyday one: *jecur* is *Hepar*, *cerebrum* is *Encephalon*, *tergum* is *Dorsum*.
- **The spacing is the vocabulary practice's**, rule for rule and number for number (Jack, 20 November 2026: in a first version outside the lexicon a card marked "Again" came back a few cards later; "do we not have a way of repeating in our other game already?"). `src/chartae.js` has a copy of the constants and of the three functions that place a card, and a test fails when it differs from `src/practice.js`. Progress is kept under a key of its own (`lexicon-chartae`), one spacing and one progress for the cards of every subject: saying a name at a picture and saying it at an English word are two questions.
- **The page is the practice's page**: the lobby, the meter, Start, the round in its layer, the two marks, the step back, Latin unless English is chosen (the same switch and the same key). The deck is chosen by subject (*Rēs* / "Subjects": *Omnia*, *Animālia*, *Plantae*, *Corpus*) and by kind; the kinds line offers the kinds of the subjects chosen, and a subject un-chosen takes its chosen kinds with it. The two lines behave as the vocabulary's: choices within a line add up, the lines narrow one another, a line with nothing chosen does not narrow, and choosing every one is choosing none. "All the cards" lists every card with its picture and name, under its subject and its kind, to look through with a child; a card in the list opens to be looked at, its back shown, with the cards before and after it, and nothing is recorded (Jack, 20 November 2026).
- **The pictures** are from Wikimedia Commons, under licences that let anyone reuse them, commercially too (CC0, public domain, CC BY, CC BY-SA; the builder refuses any other): most are Featured pictures or Quality images there. Each is cropped to 720 by 511 and kept in `src/chartae/<id>.jpg`; the builder copies them to `chartae/`. The card names the author and the licence, both linked, and says that the picture is cropped. A new card needs all of that in `data/chartae.json`; see the comment above `picture_cards` in `scripts/build.py` for the fields. `python3 scripts/fetch_picture.py <card-id> "<File name on Commons>" [--focus x,y] [--box left,top,right,bottom]` fetches a picture: it reads the licence from the file's own metadata and refuses one the builder would refuse (a file under several licences, as the Getty's are, is taken under the first the builder takes), downloads a rendition 1920 pixels wide, crops it to the cards' ratio around the focus (the centre unless given; `--box` crops to a box first), writes the JPEG, and prints the card's `image` block. Look at every picture it writes.
- **Pictures of the body** (Jack, 20 November 2026): the parts outside are shown by photographs, the parts inside by medical drawings without lettering. The rest is the coordinating session's, for the editor to judge: a nude trunk is shown by an ancient or classical marble statue, never a living person, and so is a head where a face would be the subject; no identifiable face of a living person, no children, no medical photographs of dissections or operations, and nothing gruesome, since a three-year-old looks at these; a drawing's labels, letters, and leader lines are cropped out (`--box`), or the drawing is not taken. A drawing in SVG or PNG is fetched as Wikimedia renders it, and a transparent ground becomes white.
- **A card stands for one thing**, not for a kind: there is no card "fish" or "bird" (Jack, 20 November 2026: shown a perch or a robin, nobody answers "fish" or "bird"), and no *arbor* beside *quercus*.
- **A sentence from the editor's readers is shown as "composed"**, as everywhere on the site; the data keeps its place (`Libellus V`). `scripts/check_libelli.py` does not read this file yet, so such a sentence is checked by hand when the readers change.
- **Offline.** The page, its script, and its data are saved with the rest when the lexicon installs. The pictures (several megabytes) are not: they are saved as they are read, and the page fetches those still missing once it has been opened, then says that the game works offline. Once saved they stay: the worker's limit of 600 pages does not count them and never clears them away (Jack, 20 November 2026, for whom the cards must work offline), and the page removes the pictures of cards that are gone. After new cards are published, the page has to be opened once with a connection for their pictures.

**The gap games** (*Lacūnae*, 20 November 2026): short dialogues with gaps to fill, with a hint under each gap if the reader wishes. Five games, each its own page: *Ubi est Theodōrus?* (`theodorus.html`, keys `uet-…`), the adverbs of place, in five rounds (*Ubi? Quō? Unde? Quā?* and a mixed round, *Iter*), built on a game the editor invented in conversation; *Quis pulsat?* (`quis-pulsat.html`, `qp-…`), the indefinite pronouns, in six (*Quis? Quid? Quem? Cujus? Cui? Uter?*); *Hic an ille?* (`hic-an-ille.html`, `hi-…`), the demonstrative pronouns, in seven; *Bis bīna* (`bis-bina.html`, `bb-…`), the numbers, in five, with the numbers as hints («Numerōs ostendere»); and *Heri, hodiē, crās* (`heri-hodie-cras.html`, `hc-…`), the six tenses of the indicative active, one verb a round, with the tenses as hints («Persōnās et tempora ostendere»). The editor approved the last three on 20 November 2026 ("all three please"). The name in the games is the editor's choice. One engine makes every page: `src/lacunae.html`, with a data file per game in `src/lacunae/` (the coordinating session's choice, for the editor to judge). The front page shows the five as one item, *Lacūnae*, with each game's name as a link, and the practice page gives them a line of their own (also the coordinating session's choice); both lists, and each game's foot links, are made from the files. A new game of the kind is a new data file there (its fields are described at `gap_games` in `scripts/build.py`, which refuses a malformed one), a line in `.gitignore`, and its docs. A form without macrons counts as right and is given them, unless macrons are required; *j* typed as *i* is right and is given the house spelling; "Show" keeps the reader's right forms, fills the rest, and leaves a wrong answer struck through under its gap; the score counts only the reader's own. Under the card, «Mōnstrā fōrmās» opens a table of all the forms the game drills (the editor's request and his words, 20 November 2026), from the game file's `tabulae`; in *Heri, hodiē, crās* each verb has its table, shown in its round. All the dialogues, the tables, and the Latin of the interface are Claude's wording and compilation, awaiting the editor's review. The pages are dressed as the clock is (the lexicon's colours and faces, lapis for a right answer, its theme the lexicon's, asking nothing of other sites), and their interface follows the practice page's choice of language.

All these pages are saved by the service worker with the rest, so they work offline; the picture cards' pictures as said above.

## Review

Every record is a draft until the editor has read it (Jack, 15 November 2026). The whole process is in [docs/review.md](docs/review.md); in short:

- **Only the editor reviews, and not on the site** (Jack, 15 November 2026). He reads the drafts and marks them right in a private page of his own, built by `scripts/editor_review.py`, which saves his verdicts in its own database for an agent to apply. The public site has none of his controls and never prints his name.
- **On every entry's page**, at its foot, the site offers the flag: *Notā* (Flag) opens a small box with six reasons to tap and a line to write. The back of a practice card has the same. A flag is kept on the device (`lexicon-review`) with the revision of the record it was given on.
- **`review.html`** (linked from every footer): how many entries are reviewed and how many are drafts, and *Mitte*: one report of the reader's flags not yet sent, to share, copy, or open as a GitHub issue.
- **`scripts/apply_review.py`** reads a report, the editor's or a reader's (one format; a reader's is applied with `--flags-only`, so that only the editor's verdict marks an entry reviewed): an entry marked right becomes reviewed if it has not changed since; a flag goes to `notes/review-flags.md` for an agent to act on, and sends a reviewed entry back to draft.
- **The site** says "Reviewed 15 November 2026." or "Not yet reviewed" (a link to About) at the foot of each entry, with no name, counts the reviewed entries on the front page, and ticks them quietly in the lists.

Agents never mark a record reviewed. The flag's interface is Latin unless English was chosen on the practice page.

## On a phone: the app and offline use

Opened from the home screen, a scrolled page showed through under the iPhone's clock and battery. Every page now has a strip of paper as tall as the status bar (`env(safe-area-inset-top)`) fixed at the top of the screen, and what sticks to the top sticks under it (14 November 2026). It could not be tried on a phone here; in a browser without such a bar the strip has no height.

The lexicon installs from the browser, with no app store. In Safari on an iPhone: Share, then Add to Home Screen. It then opens from its icon in a window of its own, not in a browser tab (`manifest.webmanifest`, the icons in `src/icons/`, and the tags `app_head` adds to every page).

A service worker (`src/sw.js`, built to `sw.js`) keeps the lexicon on the device: the front page, the category pages, the practice pages and their cards, the stylesheet, the scripts, the search data, and the fonts when it installs, and each entry or word page as it is read (the 600 most recent). So the lexicon opens at once, and the search works offline, showing each entry's words and examples; an entry page never opened on that device needs a connection. The worker's version is a hash of the files it keeps, so a published change replaces them: a reader sees it at the second opening after it is published. `node scripts/make_icons.mjs` redraws the icons.

Opened from the home screen of an iPhone, a link to another site (Wiktionary, Logeion) opens in a sheet inside the app, closed with one tap to come back where one was. Sending such links to Safari instead (through its `x-safari-https://` address) was tried on 13 November 2026 and taken out: the link opened in Safari and in the sheet both.
