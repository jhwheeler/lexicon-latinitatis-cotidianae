Macrons, usage and evidence

Macrons mark long vowels; short vowels are left plain. A heavy syllable is not automatically a long vowel. Inflected examples, genitives, principal parts and Latin headings are included in the review.

For ancient vocabulary, the main references are Lewis & Short and Lewis’s Elementary Latin Dictionary through Perseus and Logeion, with Dickinson’s core list for cross-checking. Hidden quantities are also compared against Bennett’s discussion with Michelson’s corrections and notes on Allen. Dictionary omissions are not automatically proof of shortness; disputed lengths are identified where relevant.

For modern words, the classical lengths of inherited roots and regular Latin endings are retained where justified. Loanword stem quantities that the checked sources do not establish are explicitly labelled. “Reading convention” marks an adopted reconstruction, rather than an attested ancient pronunciation.

Ancient Latin means the word and its relevant basic sense are ancient. Lexicographic evidence means a modern dictionary records the expression; it does not imply universal acceptance. Modern extension applies an older word to a modern object. Working formation identifies our own phrase or name adaptation.

Example sentences are composed for conversation with Theo. They are not quotations unless an author and passage are identified. Noun genitives and genders, important sense distinctions, and known alternative forms are supplied where useful.

Scratch

scabō, scabere, scābī

Ancient Latin
  • scabō, scabere, scābī — scratch or scrape; no attested supine is supplied in Lewis & Short.
  • scalpō, scalpere, scalpsī, scalptum — scratch, scrape, carve, engrave.

  • Nōlī scabere. — Don’t scratch.
  • scrībō, scrībere, scrīpsī, scrīptum — write; historically associated with scratching or incising. English script belongs to this family.

Vitamin

vītamīnum, vītamīnī n.

Neo-LatinReading convention
  • vītamīnum, vītamīnī n. — vitamin; a modern lexical form. The feminine variant vitamina, vitaminae f. is also recorded by Morgan–Owens, without documented stem quantities.
  • vītamīna, vītamīnōrum n. pl. — vitamins, using the neuter form.
  • vītamīnicus, vītamīnica, vītamīnicum — pertaining to vitamins; a transparent derivative, not claimed here to be corpus-verified.
  • For a multivitamin preparation, praeparātum plūra vītamīna continēns is an explicit description; the previous unsupported claim for multivītamīnum has been withdrawn.
  • Hic cibus multa vītamīna continet. — This food contains many vitamins.
  • The international term was coined from “vita” and “amine”; it does not imply that all vitamins are amines.

The ī in vīta and the adopted international -amīn- reading give our convention vītamīnum; these are reconstructed Neo-Latin reading quantities, not attested Classical measurements.

Adventure / venture

Ancient LatinMedieval LatinModern semantic extension
  • ausum, ausī n. — a daring attempt, venture, undertaking or enterprise; our preferred word for modern adventure when the emphasis is on something voluntarily and boldly undertaken.
