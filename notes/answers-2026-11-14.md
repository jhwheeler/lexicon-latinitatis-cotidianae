# Answers that answer the headword: the pass of 14 November 2026

The editor, practising with the flash cards (a card asks the English headword and shows the record's first recommended term), met *mountain range*, a noun, answered only by the verb phrase *perpetuīs jugīs tendere*, "stretch in an unbroken range", and took the entry for broken. A noun headword should be answered by a noun where Latin has one; where Latin really says the thing with a verb, the record must say so. The rule is in `docs/records.md` ("The answer answers the headword").

## How the records were found

`python3 scripts/answer_kinds.py` compares each record's `pos` with the kind of its first recommended term, read from the term's `grammar` or its dictionary form. Before the pass it reported 141 records, and after it 112; the first recommended terms it cannot classify (bare words, nouns with adjectives, clauses: 1,133 of them) were searched separately for clauses and finite verbs, which added the noun clauses listed at the end. Every Latin word promoted below was confirmed in Lewis & Short with `scripts/lookup.py`, or is cited from Traupman or Morgan–Owens by page or headword. No noun was made up to fill a card.

## The policy applied

- Where the record already had, or the sources have, a word of the right kind that truly answers the headword, it now leads: it is the first recommended term, and the verbal expression is an alternative (or stays recommended where the headword covers it too).
- Where Latin says the thing with a verb (or an adverb, or a genitive) and the record's own comment gives the word of the right kind as rare, late, technical or different in sense, the answer stays, and the gloss and comment say plainly that Latin uses a verb.
- Where the headword is truthfully two parts of speech, `pos` says so.

## For the editor

In these the classical idiom leads and a word of the right kind stands lower, because the record's own comment calls that word rare, late, technical or different in sense. If the card should show the word of the right kind all the same, say so and they can be switched:

- government: *quī rērum potiuntur*, "those in power"; Traupman 179, 323 and Morgan–Owens give *rēs pūblica*, classically the state itself.
- possible, impossible: *fierī potest*, *fierī nōn potest*; *possibilis* and *impossibilis* are Quintilian's, technical.
- temporary: *ad tempus*; *temporārius* is rare before Augustus (Nepos, Seneca).
- visible: *in cōnspectū esse*; *cōnspicuus* is poetic and post-Augustan, *aspectābilis* Cicero's philosophical word.
- aware: *nōn ignōrāre*; *gnārus* is rare but classical.
- immediate: *statim*; *praesēns* is "immediate" of a punishment or a payment on the spot (Cicero).
- forward: *prōgredior*; the adverb *porrō* is in the record.
- available (to be had): *suppetō*; *parābilis* is "easy to get" (Cicero; Morgan–Owens).
- compensation: *damnum sarcīre*; *satisfactiō* is reparation only from Tacitus on.
- information: *certiōrem facere*; *indicium* is a disclosure, *nūntius* news.
- treatment (of people): *tractō*; Traupman 397 gives *tractātiō*, classically of handling things.
- religious: *rēs dīvīnae*; with other nouns the record says *sacer*.
- summarize: *summātim* with a plain verb; Morgan–Owens gives *perstringō*, which needs *breviter* to be clear.

- *enter* carried the grammar line "adverb" on the verb *intrō, intrāre*; removed.
- *burglary* cited Traupman 283 for *effrāctūra*; Traupman's entries are on pp. 194 and 284 (*domūs latrōcinium*, *parietēs perfodere*, *effrāctor*, *perfossor*), and the record now cites those.
- *perpetuīs jugīs tendere*, labelled as Traupman's, is Pliny's (NH 3.48, quoted by Lewis & Short s.v. *perpetuus*).
- Several records' "composed" line under Sources is plural with one composed example (initiative, intervention, let go, priority), and some Traupman lines lack pages cited in the terms (available, important, occupy, wrong); left as found.
