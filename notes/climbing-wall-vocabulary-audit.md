# Climbing-wall vocabulary — 3 November 2026

Scope: a visit to Balkan Climbing with Theo. Six grouped entries supplement the existing playground material; general body-part and colour cards remain the canonical broader lists.

## Evidence and editorial decisions

- **scandō, scandere** is ancient: Lewis & Short cites climbing walls and other objects. The dictionary gives no perfect or supine, so the entry does not manufacture them. **ascendō** and **dēscendō** supply familiar alternatives for going up and down.
- **ansa** classically names a handle or something taken hold of. Its application to artificial climbing holds is our semantic extension. The descriptive handhold and foothold phrases are composed, not quotations or standardized sports terminology.
- **pariēs ad scandendum** was already recorded as our functional climbing-wall description in entry-179. **via** for a climbing route is an explicitly marked extension. No separate ancient technical sense of “bouldering problem” is claimed.
- **fūnis**, **nōdus**, **calceus**, **culcita**, **cingulum** and **lōrum** are ancient nouns. A padded climbing mat, climbing shoes, synthetic straps and a modern harness are contextual applications or working descriptions. In particular, **cingulum ad scandendum** is our proposed description, not an attested technical equivalent of a full modern harness.
- **prehendere** takes hold; **tenēre** continues holding. **attollere** raises a limb; its missing classical perfect/supine are left unsupplied. **nītor** takes an ablative support and can express upward effort; **Pedibus nītere** applies this to the feet. All coaching prompts are composed.
- The knee is **genū, genūs n.**, correcting the unmarked final vowel in the initial conversation. **dēsilīre** has long ī in the infinitive. Body-part vocabulary, directional contrasts and colour agreement are included in context.
- All the vocabulary and prompts from the conversation are represented in entries 285–290, or repeated with links/pointers to existing entries. Additional related terms cover knots, harnesses, straps, shoes, grasping versus holding, releasing, pulling, fear and offering assistance.
- Macrons and consonantal **j** follow the repository's conventions. Existing entries and anchors are preserved; entry-179 and entry-188 gain reciprocal links.
