# Coinages only where needed: the second pass, first half (15 November 2026)

The editor's principle (13 November 2026) is in `AGENTS.md` and `docs/records.md` ("Coinages only where needed"); the first pass and the editor's verdicts on it are in `notes/coinages-2026-11-13.md`. That pass took the coinages that stood beside an attested term. This one takes the records in which every term is ours, so that the page has no attested word at all (the editor, 15 November 2026: "the coinages as well, we should check that").

## Scope

The 219 such records were divided between two sessions. This half is the 110 records listed in `coinages-a.tsv` (acceptance testing to merge, in alphabetical order of their ids): 133 terms, all marked ours before the pass. The other half has its own notes.

## Method

Each record was read, and searched on 15 November 2026 in the reference works kept in the latinitas `refs/`, with `scripts/lookup.py` and with direct searches of the same files:

- **Traupman** and **Morgan–Owens** (the copy of the *Adumbratio* saved 4 November 2026): the English headword, each of the record's other English names, and the nearest plain words (for the bin bag: garbage, trash, rubbish; for the climbing frame: jungle gym, monkey bars). Morgan–Owens was searched in its headings and, where a heading gave nothing, in its whole text. A heading that stands in Morgan–Owens without any Latin is named as such.
- **Andresian**: the English in her word lists, the Latin stems of our terms in her picture pages, and her pages for the house, the kitchen, food, school, electronics, games, clothes, vehicles and the town read through (pp. 1–3, 5–6, 9–10, 13–17, 38–42). She has nothing for any of these records.
- **Levi–Bini**: the Italian word, where the thing existed in 1937 (*album*, *palloncino*, *fermata*, *seggiolone*, *figlioccio*, *formaggiaio*, *biglie* and others).
- **Lewis & Short**: every Latin word of our terms, for its sense and its quantities; the English in its definitions; and for phrases the whole text of its articles (which is how Cicero's *aurēs clausae*, Columella's *adstruitur recēns aedificium* and Pliny's *vitium* for a fruit's core were found).
- The loci collections, for phrases; Gellius 9.4.9 is quoted from `loci-gellius.md`.

No claim below rests on anything else. A comment that says "Traupman and Morgan–Owens have no word for …" means that those searches found none; where Levi–Bini were searched too, the comment names them.

The outcomes are the first pass's, with one case it did not meet. There, an ordinary combination of attested words was moved to the record's examples, because an attested term stood in the record. Here a record would often be left with no term at all, and the builder requires one. So such a phrase stays as the term, marked ours as the first pass left *index fūmī* (smoke alarm), *ōris tegimentum* (face mask), *tabula multiplicātiōnis* (times table) and *mēnsa pōmāria* (fruit stall), and its comment says that it is a description and what was searched. Where the record keeps one term, its other phrases of the same kind were moved to the examples as before.

A single classical word was relabelled classical only where it is used in its own sense, as the editor ruled for *mendum* and *ēmendāre* (a backup is a copy, a climbing route is a way, to merge is to join). A classical word carried over to another kind of thing (*ānsa*, a handle, for a climbing hold; *cōdex* for code) stays ours, with the reason.

## For the editor: the doubtful terms

Not changed, except that *album* is relabelled. Each line gives our word, the attested alternative with its source, and a recommendation.

- **acceptance testing**: *probātiō acceptātiōnis*. No reference work has a term, so a coinage is needed; but *acceptātiō* is not in Lewis & Short, which has *acceptiō*, "a taking, receiving, or accepting" (Cic. Top. 37; Sall. J. 29.4). Recommendation: *probātiō acceptiōnis*.
- **automated testing**: *probātiō automatica*. No reference work has a term; but *automaticus* is not in Lewis & Short. The attested adjective is *automatus* (Petr. 50.1, "self-moving, spontaneous", Lewis & Short; Traupman 371, *sclopētum automātum*, an automatic rifle; Egger's *birota automata*, in Morgan–Owens s.v. bicycle: motorcycle). Recommendation: *probātiō automata*, and the same word in the comment of manual testing.
- **album** (for photographs): *liber imāginum* is recommended; *album, albī* was marked ours, but Levi–Bini give it for the Italian *album* (p. 3, with *tabulae* and *cōdicillī*), so it is now labelled modern with that evidence. Which leads is the open question: a modern reference's word, classically a white notice board, against a transparent phrase of ours. Recommendation: recommend *album* and keep *liber imāginum* as a composed example.
- **balloon**: *vēsīca* (our extension of "bladder"; Lewis & Short gives it for things made of bladder), with *vēsīca īnflāta* and *follis īnflātus* as alternatives. Levi–Bini have *folliculus* for *palloncino* (p. 45), and in Lewis & Short *folliculus* is "a ball to play with, inflated with air" (Suet. Aug. 83). Whether Levi–Bini's *palloncino* is a toy balloon or a small ball cannot be told from the entry. Recommendation: recommend *folliculus* (classical, with Levi–Bini), keep *vēsīca* as the alternative, and move the two phrases to the examples.
- **Milan Bergamo Airport**: *āëroportus Bergomēnsis*. The town is *Bergomum* (Plin. NH 3.124) and its attested adjective *Bergomās, Bergomātis* (Lewis & Short, from inscriptions; Morgan–Owens, from Pliny). *Bergomēnsis* is in none of the works at hand; it may well be the later form (the diocese's name), but that was not read anywhere tonight. Recommendation: *āëroportus Bergomās*, unless a source for *Bergomēnsis* is read and cited.
- **child seat**: *sēdēs puerīlis*. The record doubles the car seat record (`car-seat`, headed "car seat (a child's)", which lists "child seat" among its other names and has *sellula*). No reference work has a word for either. Recommendation: merge it into car seat, as toy bear was merged into teddy bear, with *sēdēs puerīlis* as an example there.
- **core** (of an apple): the term is a whole description, *pars media mālī, in quā sēmina sunt*. Lewis & Short s.v. *vitium* has "In fruits, the useless part, the core: *vitiumque cīnctum frūctū*, Plin. 15.112", and s.v. *volva* "the seed-covering" of fruits (*pōmōrum*, Scrib. 104). Traupman and Morgan–Owens have no word. Recommendation: do not recommend *vitium* on one passage of Pliny (said aloud it means "the fault"); shorten the term to *pars media mālī*, give the rest as the example it already is, and name Pliny's word in the comment. The present comment speaks of *cor*, which nobody proposed.
- **courgette**: *cucurbitula*, our extension of a diminutive that classically names a wild gourd and a cupping glass. Morgan–Owens s.v. squash: zucchini, courgette has *cucurbita Italica* (its own phrase; no source given). Recommendation: recommend *cucurbita Italica*; keep *cucurbitula* as the alternative if it is the family's word, and say so in its comment, which now leans on the cookbook's name.

## Done, and for the editor to reverse if wrong

- **air route**: *via āëria* is deleted and Traupman's *trāmes āërius* (pp. 215, 270) entered. Traupman's English for it is "airline"; the lexicon's airline record already reads it as the route, since a *trāmes* is a path, and this follows it.
- **glide** (standing, on a balance bike): *stāns lābī* is now an example and the term is *lābor, lābī, lāpsus sum*, the classical verb for gliding (Lewis & Short; Traupman 49 and 372 uses it of rollerblading). The record now overlaps slip, which has the same verb and "glide" among its other names; the two could be one.
- **halve**: *in duās partēs secāre* is now an example. The term is the classical *in duās partēs dīvidere* (Caes. BG 3.1.6; Cic. Rep. 1.19, in Lewis & Short s.v. *dīvidō*), with Apicius's *mediāre* (3.9; "to halve, divide in the middle (post-class.)", Lewis & Short) as an alternative labelled late.
- **Relabelled classical, as words or phrases in their own sense**: *exemplar* (backup: a copy; Traupman 260 has it for a copy on a computer), *via* (climbing route), *sībilāre* (choo choo, the alternative: a train's whistle is a whistle), *conjungere* and *conjūnctiō* (merge), *aurēs clausae* (blocked ears: Cicero's words, Lael. 90, said of ears shut to the truth), *aedificium adstrūctum* (extension: the participle of Columella's *veterī adstruitur recēns aedificium*, 1.5), *putāre / arbitrārī sibi ratiōnem reddendam esse* (hold oneself accountable: Cicero's construction, Verr. 2.2.28, which was already its evidence), *charta bibula* (kitchen paper: Pliny's phrase, which Morgan–Owens gives for a paper towel). Each comment says what in the use is ours. **Relabelled modern**: *sūcus citreī* (lemon juice; Morgan–Owens has the phrase) and *album*.
- **Left as ours, though the word is classical and its sense near**: *aestimātor* (analyst), *statiō* (bus stop), *sellula* (car seat), *cubus* (building blocks). Their comments call them our use. On the precedent of *mendum* they could be relabelled classical; the application is further from the classical sense than in the cases above, so they were left.
- **curtain ring**: the term read *annulus aulaeī*. Lewis & Short has *ānulus* "(not ann-)", and the lexicon's ring record writes *ānulus*, so the term, its comment, its example and its link were corrected. The session correcting macron slips may meet the same record.
- **curtain rod**: the alternative *virga extensilis aulaeī* was deleted, not moved: *extensilis* is not in Lewis & Short.
- **cheesemonger**: the term had no genitive or gender; it is now *cāseārius, cāseāriī m.*
- **e-ticket**: its comment was about *chartāceus*, "made of paper", left over from another entry; it now speaks of the term.

## What could not be verified

- **cable reel**: the record's source line is a link "Morgan–Owens: reel" at neolatinlexicon.org, and the old comment spoke of "the recorded reel noun". The *Adumbratio* in `refs/` has no entry for a reel, and Traupman none. The line was left; the comment says only what was read (*fūsus* is a spindle in Lewis & Short). Someone should open the link. The same holds for the "Morgan–Owens: Silva" line of cycling gloves; the glove itself (*chīrothēca*) is in the *Adumbratio* and is cited from it.
- **Bergomēnsis** and Levi–Bini's ***palloncino***: see the doubtful list.
- Traupman prints *automātum* with a long *a* (p. 371); Lewis & Short has *autŏmătus*. This matters only if the editor takes *probātiō automata*.

## Noticed on the way, outside this pass

- Three terms of ours in these records still have no reason in their comment, because they are doubtful or household words and were not touched: *vēsīca* (balloon), *āëroportus Bergomēnsis*, *Bene volāte!* (whose source line says "A family phrase"). Traupman has *Bene ambulā!* for "Have a good trip!" (p. 212), which *Bene volāte!* follows; the record could say so.
- The 54 ordinary descriptions are still of period `ours`, so the site prints them with the asterisk of an unattested form, though every word in them is attested. A way of marking "a description in ordinary words" apart from "a formation of ours" would be truer; it is a change to the data format and was not made.
- `answer_kinds.py` lists two of these records, as it did before the pass: camping (a noun answered by *sub pellibus pernoctāre*; its comment says that Latin uses a verb) and choo choo (an interjection answered by the verbs *anhēlāre* and *sībilāre*). Inside out, an adverb, is answered by *tunica inversa*, "a shirt inside out"; the script does not list it. No reference work has a word, and Lewis & Short's *invertō* is "turn upside down, turn about", so nothing was changed.
- Dried fruit is *frūctūs siccī*, while the lexicon's word for fruit is *pōmum*; Morgan–Owens's dried things are *siccātus* or *passus*.
- The testing terms in this half (acceptance, automated, integration, manual) have siblings in the other half (unit test, regression testing, test case, test condition, tester); the editor's answer on *acceptātiō* and *automaticus* bears on how those read.
- Source lines that are not citations were left alone, since another session is clearing them ("No source has a word for …", "Descriptions and prompts labelled as ours …", "Looked for without result … (9 November 2026)"). Where this pass entered an attested term, no such line contradicts it.
- The comments of core and courgette (both doubtful, so untouched) need rewriting whatever the editor decides: the first speaks of *cor*, the second of the cookbook by name.
