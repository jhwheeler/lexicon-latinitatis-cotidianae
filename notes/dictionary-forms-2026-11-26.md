# Bare terms given their dictionary forms (26 November 2026)

Jack, 26 November 2026, agreed to the order proposed on the 24th: the tropics, then the bare forms, then examples. AGENTS.md asks that every term have "its full dictionary form, correctly marked: the genitive and gender of a noun, all the principal parts of a verb", because a form the dictionary form does not show is not found by the search or linked in a text.

A survey of every term found 386 nouns, verbs, and adjectives given as one bare word (*spīrāre*, *fulvus*, *mōriō*). About 120 are right so (adverbs, impersonals, indeclinables, names, inflected forms marked as such in their grammar note). The other 267 were read by two Opus researchers against Lewis & Short, and for modern words against the source the term cites (report only); Claude (Fable) vetted and applied.

## What changed

- **232 terms in 188 records** have their forms: nouns with genitive and gender (*mōriō, mōriōnis m.*), verbs with the parts Lewis & Short gives (*spīrō, spīrāre, spīrāvī, spīrātum*; two parts only where it gives no more, as *ganniō, gannīre*), adjectives with their endings (*āter, ātra, ātrum*; *mītis, mīte*; *patiēns, patientis*).
- 25 grammar notes that only repeated what the form now shows ("noun, second declension") were removed.
- 35 terms stay bare, with reason: adverbs (*fortiter*, *tūtō*), the third persons the animal-sound records give on purpose (*clāmat*, *strīdet*), *pluit*, *tepēscit*, *disserēnat*, the locative *rūrī*, the imperative *dīmitte*, *gravida* (the feminine on purpose), and *suī* in "family", which changes with the person (*meī*, *tuī*).
- pull and rest each had the same verb twice, once bare; the two are one term now.
- be-awake: *vigilō* has its perfect and supine; gloves: *digitābula* is marked plural.
- The words in the texts that find their entry rose from 125,348 to 125,483.

## Word pages that moved

A word's page takes its address from the headword, so 58 addresses are gone and 40 are new: `l/aberrare.html` is now `l/aberro.html`, and where the first person already had a page (*captō*) the two are one. No record's address changed. `data/families.json` names *spīrō* where it named *spīrāre*.

## Passive headwords

Where the passive is the point, the term is written from the passive, as the lexicon already wrote *invehor* and *vehor*: *tondeor, tondērī, tōnsus sum* (get a haircut), *colōror* (get a tan), *gelor*, *glacior* (freeze), *glomeror* (go lumpy), *scindor* (get torn), *vehor* (ride).

## For Jack to judge

- **A macron changed:** *perēdō* is now *peredō, peredere, perēdī, perēsum* (Lewis & Short *pĕr-ĕdo*; its *perēdō* is another verb).
- **cinema:** *cinēmatēum* keeps its short *i* (Greek κίνημα); Traupman prints *cīnēmatēum*, and the same record's *theātrum cīnēmaticum* has the long vowel. One of the two should give way.
- **t-shirt:** *camīsula* (Morgan–Owens's long *ī*) stands beside *camisiola*; Lewis & Short has *camĭsia*.
- **drill:** *furfuraculum* was given regular forms, but the word could not be found in the source its evidence names.
- **Forms no source prints**, given as the ending suggests: *mangus, mangī m.*; *neonātus, neonātī m.*; *radiophōnium*; *chronometrum*; *tomātum*; *lavandārium* (neuter); *titina*, *cūnābulāria*, *īnfantāria*; *periēgētēs, periēgētae m.*
- *ringor, ringī, rictus sum* keeps the short *i* that Lewis & Short and de Vaan leave unmarked.
- *plicō* has *plicāvī (plicuī), plicātum (plicitum)*, as the lexicon writes *applicō*; Lewis & Short has those perfects on Priscian's word only.
