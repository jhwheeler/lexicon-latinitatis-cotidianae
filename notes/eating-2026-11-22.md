# Eating: the sweep of 22 November 2026

Jack's second category (22 November 2026), after getting around: eating in general, at home and out. That is the family table, cooking and the kitchen, and restaurants and cafés. About 420 English phrases were checked against the lexicon; some 195 were already answered, and the gaps were researched in five groups (at the table; cooking; quantities and the kitchen; eating out; drinks, diets, complaints, and taste). The research was done by report-only agents and vetted by the coordinating session, as in `notes/getting-around-2026-11-22.md`; nothing here is Jack's review.

53 records are new and 73 gained English wordings, examples, terms, or all three. All are drafts.

## How it was done

- As in the getting-around sweep: the authors first (Lewis & Short, the loci collections, the Latin Library texts), then Traupman, Morgan–Owens, and Levi–Bini for modern things, then a plain description.
- Much comes from the comic cooks and parasites (Plautus's *Pseudolus*, *Aulularia*, *Stichus*, *Menaechmi*; Terence's *Adelphoe*), Cato, Columella, Petronius's dinner, Horace's *Satires*, Martial, and Juvenal. Apicius is used for the verbs of cooking and is labelled late.
- Every quotation was matched word for word, by script, against the Latin Library texts or found in the loci tables. Where the Latin Library's reading differs from the loci table's (Ter. Haut. 211, *quid cēnae siet*), the loci table's stands.
- The inscription under "the bill, please" (*Cōpō, computēmus*, CIL IX 2689, the innkeeper's stone from Aesernia) was read in the Epigraphische Datenbank Heidelberg (HD000649).
- The school colloquies were not used.

## Terms that are ours

Nothing attested was found for these, so a description or a sentence of ours leads, with what was searched in its comment:

- cling film: *membrāna plastica*; kitchen foil: *bractea alūminiī*
- compliments to the chef: *Coquum meīs verbīs laudā.* (on Cicero's *meīs verbīs*)
- creamy: *mollis et pinguis*
- dish of the day: *ferculum hodiernum*; set menu: *cēna certō pretiō*
- do you have a table? *Estne vōbīs mēnsa vacua?*
- don't talk with your mouth full: *Nōlī loquī, dum mandis.*
- eat in or take away? *Hīcine edēs an auferēs?*
- food court: *forum popīnārum*
- gluten-free: *glūtinis expers*
- inside or outside? *Intus an sub dīvō?* (*sub dīvō* is Cicero's)
- junk food: *cibus īnsalūbris*
- overcooked: *nimis coctus*
- room temperature: *nec calidus nec frīgidus* (Seneca's words, Ep. 67.1)
- the same for me: *Idem mihi, quaesō.*

## For Jack to judge

- **chew**: the record's answer is still *mandūcō*. Classical *mandō, mandere* (Cicero) was added as an alternative; by the rule that a late word never replaces a good classical one it should probably lead, but that changes an existing answer.
- **beat the eggs / crack an egg**: *ōva dissolvere* and *ōva frangere* are Apicius's, and late; nothing classical was found.
- **ingredients**: *impēnsa* is post-classical, and in Apicius it is the prepared mixture more than its parts.
- **enjoy your meal**: *Libenter cēnā!* is Egger's modern wish (Morgan–Owens), in classical words; Horace's *Vēscere, sōdēs* presses food on a guest.
- **the bill, please**: *Computēmus.* (the Aesernia inscription; Statius).
- **split the bill**: *symbolam dare* is the comic word (Plautus, Terence); *sēparātim solvere* is the plain prose, composed.
- **it's on me**: *Dē meō.* (Plaut. Trin. 328–329; Ter. Ad. 117).
- **keep the change**: *Tibi habē.* is always dismissive in the authors; its use for change is ours.
- **cook until soft**: Cato's *coquere ūsque dōnec commadeat* leads; *commadeō* is in that one passage only.
- **best-before date**: *terminus*, on Morgan–Owens's "expiration date"; the weakest of the nouns.
- **oven glove**: *manicae* (Pliny's sleeves that covered the hands), stretched.
- **ice-cream cone**: *cōnus* and *globulus*, classical words used of a modern thing.
- **home-made**: *inēmptus*, "unbought" (Vergil, Horace), is poetic; at table one says *domī coctus*.
- **thaw**: *regelō* (Columella, Martial) was added and may deserve to lead over *liquefaciō*.
- **adustus** is written with a short *u*, by the house ruling on *ustus*; the loci table for Terence and the record for salty still have *adūstum*.

## Left out

- A Horace line where the cup is a witch's potion, a Plautus line where the dinner is a beating, and a Martial complaint that was not about a wrong order.
- *lentus* for chewy, a Vulgate verse whose book the lexicon has no settled way of citing (1 Samuel), and anything cited from memory.
- Not done: "unsalted" as a record of its own (*īnsulsus*), "open (of a shop)", and the source lines of extended records that still name the readers or the cookbook.
