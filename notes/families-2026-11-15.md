The request is Jack's (13 November 2026; 15 November: "And related words, yeah, let's try and do all those things"): the page for *acrobat* gave *petaurista* and did not lead to *petaurum*, which the lexicon has under *trampoline*. This is the first half of "Related words, properly" in the README's to-do list, the families. The method, its lists and its thresholds are Claude's, for Jack to judge. The rules for whoever changes it are in `docs/records.md`, "Word families".

## The method in a page

Every head of one word whose dictionary form the linker can read (`link_stems`) is a word for the families; so is every adverb. A head is several words when its terms have different main stems or classes (*volō, velle* and *volō, volāre*). Each word has stems with their vowel lengths: a noun's or adjective's from the genitive, a verb's present stem without its vowel and its supine stem, and for a noun of the third, fourth or fifth declension its nominative.

A word B is proposed as derived from a word A when

- a stem of B is a stem of A with a suffix of `FAMILY_SUFFIXES`, the suffix being allowed on A's kind of word and making B's kind;
- or B's stem is a prefix of `FAMILY_PREFIXES` before A's stem: for a verb, of the same conjugation, with the first vowel as it is or weakened, and agreeing in the supine too where both give one; for a noun or adjective, of the same kind and unchanged;
- or B's stem is A's supine stem in another kind of word (*cursus*, *dictum*, the frequentative *cantō*), or A's stem as a verb in *-āre* or *-īre* (*cūrō* from *cūra*).

One word under two heads is joined first: the same stem in the same declension or conjugation (*fīnēs*, *fīnis*; *vincor*, *vincō*), the same stem in two words under one concept (*cerva*, *cervus*), an adjective used as a noun under its own head.

Then a word is joined to its proposed bases only if all of them are, or come to be, in one family. A proposal from a root of two letters joins nothing but counts as one of the bases. A name is derived only from names, a common word from common words. `data/families.json` takes proposals away (`apart`), joins by hand (`together`) and splits a head whose two words have the same forms (`homographs`); two words listed apart are never brought into one family by a later join.

## Every family read once

After the first changes every family the method proposed (1,345 then) was read, and each one that looked wrong or doubtful was looked up. About 75 had a wrong join. What came of it:

- **Changes to the method.** A suffix is put only on the kind of word it is mostly made from (*nāvālis* is of *nāvis*, not of the adjective *nāvus*; *fortitūdō* of *fortis*, not of *fors*). *-or* and *-idus* only from the second conjugation (*color* is not of *colō*, nor *frīgidus* of *frīgō*). *-ium* from a verb, but not *-ia* (*venia* is not of *veniō*). *-ūtus* and *-ītus* taken out (they collide with participles: *solūtus*, *minūtus*). An exact base is preferred to a weakened one (*allevō* is of *levō*, not of *lavō*), and a base that agrees in the supine to one that does not (*adhibeō* is of *habeō*, not of *hebeō*). A proposal from a two-letter root counts against another (*redoleō*). One word under two heads is joined before anything is derived from it, or its derivatives waited for both and got neither.
- **Pairs listed apart**, each with Lewis & Short's derivation in its note. By what made them:

## The second sample: 100 fresh families of the final method

`python3 scripts/family_sample.py --seed 2 --count 100 --exclude notes/families-sample-1.txt`, drawn from the 1,185 families that share no word with the first sample; `notes/families-sample-2.txt` lists them. It was drawn before the entry for *lustrō* and *lustrum* was added to `data/families.json`: take that entry out to draw the same hundred again.

**One family of the hundred was wrong**: *lustra, lustrō, lustrum, perlustrō*. Lewis & Short has *lustro* from [2. lustrum], the purification, and the lair is 1. *lustrum*. The method joined them because the record for "explore" writes *lustrō* and *perlustrō* with a short *u*, where "look over" and "browse" have *lūstrō* and *perlūstrō*. The pair is now listed apart; the cause is in the records (below).

The other 99 were right. Read with a second look: *apium* with *apis* (L&S *apium* [apis]), *fera* with *ferus*, *mōlior* from *mōlēs* (L&S [moles]), *scrūtor* from *scrūta* (L&S [scruta]), *contextus* with *textus* (both of *texō*), *ampullor* from *ampulla* (L&S [ampulla, II]).

## Families kept that are open to doubt

They rest on Lewis & Short as read, and a reader may judge otherwise:

- *liceor*, *licet*, *licitus*, *licentia*: L&S s.v. *licet*: "v. 1. liceo".
- *mēnsa* and *mēta* with *mētior*: L&S gives both the root "ma-, measure".
- *dēbeō*, *dēbilis*: L&S has both from *habeō* (*de-habeo*, *de-habilis*); the method joined them by the look of the stem.
- *comes*, *comitia*: L&S has both from *com-* and *eō*.
- *crīmen*, *discrīmen*: L&S has both from *cernō*.
- *ignōscō*, *ignōtus*: both of *(g)nōscō* in L&S; the adjective is not the verb's participle.
- *aestīvus* with *aestus*: L&S *aestivus* [aestas], and *aestus* "kind. with aestas".
- *absurdus* with *surdus*: de Vaan s.v. *surdus* ("regularly belongs to surdus"); L&S derives it otherwise.
- *explōrō* with *plōrō*: de Vaan s.v. *plōrō* lists it; L&S prints *ex-plōro* without a derivation.
- *rēgula* with *rēx*: L&S *regula* [rego] and *rex* [rego].
- *apiārium* with *apium*: both are of *apis* (L&S), which the hand-made family now holds.

## What the method misses on purpose

- **Roots of two letters**: *agilis* and *agmen* with *agō*, *amābilis* with *amō*, *ūsus* with *ūtor*, the compounds of *stō* and *ruō* given without a supine. Each wants an entry in `together`, with its authority.
- **The 89 words whose bases are in several families** (`python3 scripts/family_sample.py --ambiguous`): *contentus* (of *contendō* or of *contineō*: truly two words), *attentus*, *prōditiō*, *invīsus*, *crēta*, *arcārius* (*arca*, *arx*, *arcus*), *pugnō* (*pugna*, *pugnus*), *portō*, *multō*, *sitiō*, *līmōsus*, *malāria*, and others. Some are one `apart` entry away from a right family.
- **A changed stem**: diminutives in *-ellus* and *-illus* (*libellus*, *castellum*, *puella* from *puer*), nouns in *-or* from verbs not of the second conjugation (*amor*, *clāmor*, *error*), adjectives in *-idus* likewise (*cupidus*, *rapidus*), inchoatives from the third conjugation, *vinculum*-like nouns from the fourth, agent nouns in *-ō, -ōnis* (*errō*, *bibō*).
- **Weakening in nouns and adjectives** (*difficilis*, *īnsulsus*, *biennium*), but for the three joined by hand.
- **Feminines and neuters beside a masculine** where no concept is shared: *fīlia* and *domina* are joined by hand; *equa* and *cerva* are joined to the masculine because they are entered under one concept with it; *monacha* and *monachus*, which are not, stay apart.
- **A name from a common word**: *Quīntīlis* from *quīntus*, *Colōnia* from *colōnus*, *austrālis* from *Auster*.
- **Compounds whose second part is not a headword**: *cōnspiciō* and *aspiciō* are not joined to each other, nor to *spectō*, since *speciō* has no entry; so *dēspectō* and *circumspectō*, which could be of either, are left out.
- **Phrases**, and heads the linker cannot read.

## The build

The families cost about 1.2 seconds of the build: 14.0 to 14.4 seconds of processor time for `build_site` before, 15.2 to 15.9 after, measured on the same machine in turn. By the clock the whole command took 17.6 to 24.6 seconds before and 20.1 to 25.8 after, while five other sessions were at work on two cores; on a quiet machine that is about 15 seconds before and 16.5 after.

## Doing it again

- `python3 scripts/family_sample.py --list`: every family, one per line. Keep the output and compare after adding records.
- `python3 scripts/family_sample.py --seed N --count 100`: a sample with Lewis & Short's bracketed derivation beside each word; `ok` marks a word whose derivation names another of the family, `?` one to look up. The mark is a help, not a verdict.
- `python3 scripts/family_sample.py --word vinculum`: one family and how each word was joined, or why a word was left out.
