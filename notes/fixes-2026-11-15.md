# Two decisions and the plain obscenities (15 November 2026)

## Seeing is believing

Jack: the classical version is better. In `seeing-is-believing` Plautus’s *Plūris est oculātus testis ūnus quam aurītī decem* (Truc. 489) is now the recommended term. Traupman’s *Vidē et crēde* stays as an alternative, with the comment that we have found no ancient source for it.

## Tired

No change of order: *fessus* stays first. The comment on *lassus* could be read as saying that *fessus* is rare, because it reported that Plautus and Terence do not have it. It now says that this shows the register of comedy, not that *fessus* is rare or wrong. *fessus* has a gloss, two citations from Lewis & Short and a comment: the standard classical word, in prose and in verse.

## The plain obscenities

Jack: “I suppose that they’re useful for some people.” Entered soberly, each with the register *vōx obscēna*, a plain English gloss, and Lewis & Short’s citations. Where Lewis & Short glosses in Latin or evasively, the comment quotes what it says and gives the ground for the plain sense.

| English | Latin | Where | Ground for the sense |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| sodomise | *paedīcō (pēdīcō)* | new record `sodomise`, adult | Lewis & Short: “to practise unnatural vice”, with *puerum* as object; Forcellini on Mart. 11.99.2, quoted by Morgan–Owens. |
| fellate | *fellō*; *irrumō*; *fellātor* | new record `fellate`, adult | Lewis & Short: *fellō* “to suck … obsc.”; *irrumō* “to give suck; hence in mal. part.”, and “to treat in a foul or shameful manner”. The sense of *irrumō* is drawn from these, not stated there. |
| masturbate | *masturbor* | new record `masturbate`, adult | Lewis & Short (“to defile one’s self”); Morgan–Owens. |
| clitoris | *clītoris*; *landīca* | new record `clitoris`, adult | Morgan–Owens. Lewis & Short has *landīca* only from a glossary. |
| penis | *verpa* | added to `penis` | Lewis & Short: *= membrum virīle*. |
| testicles | *cōleī* | added to `testicle` | Lewis & Short s.v. *culleus*: “the scrotum”; Petr. 44.14. |
| passive partner | *pathicus* | added to `catamite`, adult | Lewis & Short: “who submits to unnatural lust”. |

`penis` and `testicle` are not marked for adults; they already held *mentula* (as `vagina` holds *cunnus* and `anus` *cūlus*). If the obscene terms should be kept from children, those records need the mark or the terms a record of their own.

Left out, as less common or not established from the references here: *cēveō* and *crīsō* (Lewis & Short: “to move the haunches”), *glūbō* in its obscene sense, *cunnilingus*, *tribas*, *fututor*, *irrumātor*, the noun *paedīcō*, *verpus* (a circumcised man), and *muttō*, which Lewis & Short does not have.
