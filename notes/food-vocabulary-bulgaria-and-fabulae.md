# Bulgarian and fabulae food vocabulary

Added 29 October 2026. Four grouped entries (190–193) cover the legumes, the garden vegetables of *Theodōrus et pater* Ep. VII, the orchard and melon fruits, and the Bulgarian table (sirene, kashkaval, banitsa, tarator, lyutenitsa). The starting question was the word for *pea*, which the lexicon lacked; the fabulae and *Culīna Theodōrī* were then swept for food words not yet recorded here.

## Sources actually consulted

- Lewis & Short, the Perseus TEI XML (PerseusDL/lexica, `lat.ls.perseus-eng2.xml`), searched locally for every headword added: *pisum, phaselus, faba, lens, cicer, siliqua, legumen, caepa, porrum, brassica, cucurbitula, piperitis, cerasus/cerasum, Persae (Persicum), Armenia (Armeniacum), Cydonia (cydonia/cotonea), pepo, melopepo, melo, castanea, placenta, muria, laridum, oryza*. Entry links point to Alatius.
- The project's verified Catō loci table (library artifact *Theodōrus et pater*, `loci/cato.html`): Agr. 156, 1 (*brassica est quae omnibus holeribus antistat*), Agr. 7, 4 (*conduntur … viridēs in muriā*), and the full Agr. 76 *placenta* recipe (there marked ūsus: Culīna).
- *Theodōrus et pater*, Epp. I–VII and *Culīna Theodōrī*, text extracted from the published PDFs: cicer and the cēna rūstica (Ep. III); the market's lēns, faba, cicer, castaneae (Ep. IV); pitta cum cucurbitulīs, melongēnīs, piperītidibus (Ep. VI); Baba's garden — lycopersica, piperītidēs, cucumerēs, phasēlī, caepae, cucurbitae (Ep. VII); pōma terrestria, caepa, porrum, piperītis rubra, cucurbitula, castaneae tostae, olīvae conditae, placenta (Culīna).
- John C. Traupman, *Conversational Latin for Oral Proficiency*: pp. 71–72 are cited for *piperītis* (fresh pepper) and *pōmum terrestre / pulticula ex pōmīs terrestribus* as the *Culīna*'s own glosses already cite them; the PDF itself was not re-inspected for this pass.

## Decisions and flags

- **pīsum has a long ī.** L&S marks *pīsum* (late collateral *pīsa, -ae*); French *pois* confirms the long vowel. An earlier suggestion in conversation of a short *i* (after Greek πίσον) was wrong and is superseded.
- **pepō, peponis.** L&S prints *pĕpo, -ŏnis* but *mēlŏpĕpo, -ōnis*. The shared Greek oblique stem (πέπων, -ονος) is short; both entries here write the oblique **-pepon-** without a macron. *mēlo, mēlōnis* follows L&S as printed.
- **phasēlus** is the series' bean (Ep. VII *phasēlōs*); the entry notes that the modern bean is the American *Phaseolus* wearing the old name. *faba* is kept distinct as the broad bean.
- **cucurbitula**: the attested classical senses are a wild gourd (Scrib.) and Celsus's cupping-glass; the courgette sense is the series' own diminutive use and is labelled as ours.
- **melongēna** and **citrullus** are medieval/botanical, labelled so; no quantities are claimed for *citrullus*.
- **caepa** is the headword spelling (so the series); *cēpa* and neuter *caepe* noted.
- **Bulgarian dishes** (sirene, kashkaval, banitsa, tarator, lyutenitsa) are given transparent descriptions, not coinages. *placenta* (Catō Agr. 76) is offered for banitsa as a genuinely layered ancient analogue; the note is explicit that баница is a Slavic word and only the Romanian *plăcintă* family continues *placenta*.
- Bulgarian equivalents are given in Cyrillic throughout these entries, as the readers' glosses already do for тесла.
