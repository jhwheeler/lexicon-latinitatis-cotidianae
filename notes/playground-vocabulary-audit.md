## Coverage and evidence

### 176: Playground / play / toy / toys

- **playground: ārea lūsōria; āreae lūsōriae f.** — Traupman pp. 53, 361; Morgan–Owens also records campus lūsōrius and locus lūsōrius parvulōrum.
- **toy; toys: lūdibrium, lūdibriī n.; lūdibria, lūdibriōrum n. pl.** — Traupman p. 396 explicitly gives toy. Ancient lūdibrium often means mockery/plaything; do not assume the neutral modern toy sense in every ancient occurrence. The second stem i has variable quantity in Lewis & Short; left unmarked here, following Traupman.
- **play; game: lūdō, lūdere, lūsī, lūsum; lūdus, lūdī m.** — Ancient words. Lūdāmus! — Let’s play! Eāmus ad āream lūsōriam. — Let’s go to the playground.
- **equipment; toy version: īnstrūmentum, īnstrūmentī n.; lūsōrius, lūsōria, lūsōrium** — Our productive toy descriptions below use lūsōrius; each full compound is labelled as composed unless independently recorded.

Sources: https://neolatinlexicon.org/latin/playground/ · https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?l=ludibrium · https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?l=ludus · https://paulatinygriego.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/conversational-latin-for-oral-proficiency-traupman.pdf

### 177: Slide / slide down

- **slide (apparatus): tractus lūbricus; tractūs lūbricī m.** — Morgan–Owens explicitly records the chute sense, citing Levine. This is not the photographic slide sense.
- **slide down: dēlābor, dēlābī; lābor, lābī, lāpsus sum** — Ancient motion verbs applied to the apparatus. Dēlābere! — Slide down! Iterum dēlābī vīs? — Do you want to slide down again?
- **top; bottom: pars summa; pars īma** — Our contextual descriptions of the top and bottom of the slide. Prīmum ascende, deinde dēlābere. — First climb up, then slide down.

Sources: https://neolatinlexicon.org/latin/slide/ · https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?l=delabor · https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?l=labor

### 178: Seesaw / merry-go-round / roundabout

- **seesaw: tollenō, tollenōnis m.** — Recorded by Morgan–Owens for the playground seesaw, with eighteenth-century/Helfer evidence; the older noun also names a lifting beam.
- **merry-go-round; carousel: circumvectābulum, circumvectābulī n.** — Traupman p. 345; Morgan–Owens lists alternatives including orbis volūbilis (lūsōrius). Applied here to a small playground roundabout; not necessarily a motorized fairground ride.
- **turn; spin: vertō, vertere; circumagō, circumagere** — Ancient verbs. Lentius circumage. — Turn it more slowly. Satis est; cōnsiste. — That’s enough; stop. Composed prompts.

Sources: https://neolatinlexicon.org/latin/park__see-saw/ · https://neolatinlexicon.org/latin/merry-go-round_carousel/ · https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?l=tolleno · https://paulatinygriego.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/conversational-latin-for-oral-proficiency-traupman.pdf

### 179: Climbing frame / monkey bars / climbing wall / rope net / balance beam / zip line / spring rider

- **climbing frame: compāgēs ad scandendum** — Our functional description, a framework for climbing; not a verified fixed technical term.
- **monkey bars: compāgēs ex quā manibus pendēmus** — Our description: a framework from which we hang by our hands. Ferrum trānsversum names an individual crossbar.
- **climbing wall: pariēs ad scandendum** — Our description: a wall for climbing.
- **rope; climbing net: fūnis, fūnis m.; rēte, rētis n.** — Ancient rope/net nouns; rēte ad scandendum is our climbing-net description.
- **balance beam: trabs ad aequilībrium exercendum** — Our functional description; trabs, trabis f. is a beam.
- **zip line: fūnis dēclīvis cum sēde mōbilī** — Our description of the sloping cable and moving seat, not an established dictionary name.
- **spring rider / rocker: sella lūsōria quae hūc illūc movētur** — Our broad description of a moving play seat. It does not specifically name the spring mechanism; no exact sourced technical headword verified.

Sources: https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?l=compages · https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?l=scando · https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?l=funis · https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?l=rete · https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?l=trabs

### 180: Sandbox / sandpit / bucket / shovel / rake / sieve / mould / watering can

- **sandbox; sandpit: locus harēnā plēnus ad lūdendum** — Our description: a place full of sand for playing. Harēna, harēnae f. — sand.
- **bucket; little bucket: situla, situlae f.; sitella, sitellae f.** — Ancient container nouns applied to sand toys.
- **shovel; spade: pāla, pālae f.** — Recorded by Morgan–Owens; a child’s pāla lūsōria is our descriptive toy qualification.
- **rake: rāstellus, rāstellī m.** — Morgan–Owens records rastellus; the inherited quantity follows the rāstrum family.
- **sieve: crībrum, crībrī n.** — Recorded by Morgan–Owens; applied to the sand sieve.
- **sand mould: fōrma ad harēnam fingendam** — Our purpose phrase based on fōrma, fōrmae f., mould/form.
- **watering can: nassiterna, nassiternae f.** — Ancient water vessel; modern watering-can application is recorded by Morgan–Owens/Traupman. Stem quantity not independently established here; left unmarked.

### 182: Toy tractor / digger / excavator / bulldozer / truck / dump truck / crane / trailer

- **tractor; toy tractor: tractrum, tractrī n.; tractrum lūsōrium** — Tractor is explicitly in Traupman p. 397. Toy qualification is ours. Morgan–Owens also records māchina vectōria.
- **digger; excavator: māchina fossōria; māchinae fossōriae f.** — Morgan–Owens records the machine sense. Fossor, fossōris m. is the person digging. Māchina fossōria lūsōria is our toy phrase.
- **bulldozer: māchina aggerandī** — Recorded by Morgan–Owens, citing Levine/Helfer. The construction conveys a machine for heaping earth.
- **truck; toy truck: vehiculum onerārium; vehiculī onerāriī n.** — Recorded by Morgan–Owens. Add lūsōrium for our toy application.
- **dump truck: vehiculum onerārium quō harēna effunditur** — Our functional description, not a verified specialized tipper-truck headword.
- **crane; toy crane: grūs, gruis f.; grūs lūsōria** — Ancient crane-machine noun, applied to the toy.
- **trailer; loader: carrus quī trahitur; māchina quae harēnam in vehiculum congerit** — Our descriptions: a cart that is pulled; a machine that loads sand into a vehicle. No exact technical loader headword verified.

### 183: Ride-on car / balance bike / tricycle / scooter / wheelbarrow

- **toy car; ride-on car: autoraeda lūsōria** — Our toy qualification of the family’s established autoraeda. For a ride-on, explicitly: autoraeda lūsōria in quā puer sedēre potest.
- **balance bike: birota sine pedālibus** — Our description, a bicycle without pedals. Bicycle parts remain in the travel section.
- **tricycle: vehiculum tribus rotīs īnstrūctum** — Our transparent three-wheeled vehicle description.
- **kick scooter: vehiculum birotum quod pede prōpellitur** — Our precise description of a two-wheeled vehicle propelled by the foot. Morgan–Owens’s broader scooter headword alone does not establish every modern kick-scooter subtype.
- **wheelbarrow: vehiculum ūnirotum; vehiculī ūnirotī n.** — Recorded by Morgan–Owens. Toy qualification lūsōrium is ours.
- **ride; push; pull; load: vehī; impellere; trahere; onerāre** — Ancient verbs used for toy vehicles. Tractrum trahis. — You’re pulling the tractor.

### 184: Ball / football / basketball / goal / basket / bat / throw / catch / kick / bounce

- **ball; inflatable ball: pila, pilae f.; follis, follis m.** — Ancient ball nouns; modern sports need context. Pilā lūdere — play ball.
- **goal; basket; bat: porta, portae f.; canistrum, canistrī n.; clāva, clāvae f.** — Modern sport applications of old nouns.
- **throw; catch; kick: iaciō, iacere; excipiō, excipere; pede pulsāre** — Pilam mihi iace. — Throw me the ball. Pilam excipe. — Catch the ball.
- **bounce the ball; roll the ball: pilam ad terram iacere ut resiliat; pilam volvere** — Our descriptions distinguish throwing so that it rebounds from rolling. Resiliō, resilīre — spring/bounce back.
- **football; basketball: pilā pedibus lūdere; follis canistrīque lūdus** — Our description of playing ball with the feet; the latter is in the Vatican’s public selection. A casual ball game need not be a formal sport.

## Boundaries

Traupman directly confirms ārea lūsōria (53, 361), lūdibrium (396), tractrum (397), circumvectābulum (345), and the earlier oscillum/oscillāre entries. Morgan–Owens supplies the exact linked equipment and vehicle headwords. Most specialized frame, sandbox, zip-line, spring-rider, loader and dump-truck descriptions are ours; they are not dictionary attestations. Vatican basketball wording is a recorded modern expression. No exact ancient name for a modern plastic toy subtype is asserted. Ancient crepundia especially denotes rattles. The variable second vowel of ludibrium is left unmarked; no universally agreed modern toy terminology is claimed. Modern loan gummi receives no invented stem quantity.
