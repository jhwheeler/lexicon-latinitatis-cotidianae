# The tropics, and some stragglers (26 November 2026)

Jack, 24 November 2026, shown a sample of eight further areas: the lexicon answered 274 of 315 words, and the one real hole was the tropics. 26 November: "Great, let's do that." 149 phrases of life in the tropics were checked against the lexicon; 81 were answered. Three Opus researchers took the 68 that were not, with 24 stragglers from the other areas (report only); Claude (Fable) vetted and applied.

48 records are new and 37 gained wordings, examples, or terms. All are drafts.

## What the ancients had

Pliny knew more of this than one expects, and his words lead: the banyan is *fīcus Indica* (NH 12.22), bamboo *harundō Indica* (16.162), the banana tree *pāla* (12.24), the monsoon wind *Hippalus* (6.100, 104), coral *cūralium* (32.21–22), the water lily *nymphaea*, the orchid *orchis*, the sea turtle *testūdō marīna*, the dugout canoe *linter* (6.105). Rice is *orȳza*; the rice field is Morgan–Owens's *orȳzētum*. The Milky Way is Cicero's *orbis lacteus* and Ovid's *via lactea*; an eclipse is *dēfectiō*; a comet *comētēs* or *stēlla crīnīta*.

Pliny's *Natural History* was read in the Perseus text (Mayhoff), which a researcher fetched because the Latin Library copy stops at book 5. Every quotation of his not marked "Lewis & Short" was matched against it by script. One reading differs: at 9.5 the Perseus file has *tune*, plainly for *tunc*.

## Botanical names

For plants the Romans did not know and no reference work names, the term is the botanists' binomial, labelled modern: dragon fruit (*Selēnicēreus undātus*), passion fruit (*Passiflōra edūlis*), jackfruit (*Artocarpus heterophyllus*), durian (*Durio zibethīnus*), frangipani (*plumeria*). No local word was Latinised. The examples say *pōmum Artocarpī*, *pōmum passiflōrae*.

## Terms that are ours

coconut water: *aqua nucis Indicae*; mosquito repellent: *unguentum contrā culicēs*; satay: *carō in verubus tosta*; shoe rack: *pluteus calceōrum*; bunk bed: *lectī superpositī*; dragon fruit, as an alternative: *pōmum dracōnis*.

Classical words used of a thing the Romans did not have, labelled classical with the sense in the gloss: sarong, *carbasus* (Curtius 8.9, of Indian dress); gong, *aes*; outrigger, *linter*; flower offering, *corōna flōrea* (Plautus, Aul. 385–387, a garland for the household god); reef, *dorsum* (Verg. A. 1.110); gravity, *gravitās*.

## Calls for Jack to judge

- **Dragon fruit, jackfruit, durian.** A binomial is hard to say at table. For dragon fruit the plain *pōmum dracōnis* (the fruit's name in English and in Indonesian) stands as the alternative; should it lead? Jackfruit and durian have nothing else.
- **Papaya and guava** are written *papaja* and *guajāva*, Morgan–Owens's words with the house's j.
- **Rainy and dry season.** No set classical phrase. *imbrēs*, "the rains", leads rainy season, with *hiems*; *tempora sicca* (Tibullus) leads dry season, with *aestās* and *siccitās*.
- **Coral.** *cūralium* leads, with the long *ū* of Ovid's verse (Met. 15.416); Lewis & Short's headword is *coralium* and marks both vowels common.
- **Water buffalo** is a record of its own, *būbalus*: the buffalo record leads with *ūrus*, the aurochs.
- **Picture frame.** *corōna* is Levi–Bini's alone and could be heard as a garland.
- **Dustman.** *pūrgātor* is post-classical, and the sense is ours.
- **Cushion** is under pillow (*pulvīnus*); the record might be renamed "pillow, cushion".
