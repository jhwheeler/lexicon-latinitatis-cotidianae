# Reference data

`lewis-short-headwords.tsv` lists the headwords of Lewis & Short, *A Latin Dictionary* (1879), as the Perseus Digital Library prints them, with the macrons and breves of their orthography: one line per entry, `key<TAB>headword`. It is extracted from `CTS_XML_TEI/perseus/pdllex/lat/ls/lat.ls.perseus-eng1.xml` in [PerseusDL/lexica](https://github.com/PerseusDL/lexica); the dictionary is in the public domain, and the Perseus encoding is licensed CC BY-SA 3.0 US, so this extract is shared under the same licence, with credit to the Perseus Project, Tufts University. `scripts/check_quantities.py` uses it to compare the lexicon's vowel lengths with Lewis & Short wherever Lewis & Short marks them.

`lewis-short-ids.tsv` comes from the same Perseus file, under the same licence: `key<TAB>ids`, the numbers of the entries with each key (`soror` is `44798`; homographs such as `jus` have two). Latinitium's dictionaries address an article by number alone, and its Lewis & Short keeps these numbers, so `scripts/build.py` uses the table to link each Latin word's page to its article there (`latinitium.com/latin-dictionaries/?t=lsn44798`). To remake it, take each `<entryFree id="n…" key="…">` of the Perseus file, drop the homograph digit from the key and the `n` from the id.

`ngsl-1.01.tsv` is the New General Service List 1.01 (Charles Browne, Brent Culligan and Joseph Phillips, 2013): its 2,801 headwords with their frequency ranks, taken from the spreadsheet the authors published (as redistributed in [antdurrant/word.lists](https://github.com/antdurrant/word.lists)). It is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0. The site now offers version 1.2, which differs by a few words. `scripts/coverage.py` measures the lexicon against it; the plan's other core list, the MacArthur-Bates CDI, is a sold form, so it is used only outside the repository.

`swadesh-207.tsv` is Morris Swadesh's list of 207 basic meanings (1952, revised 1971) in its usual English wording, each with its part of speech: `word<TAB>part of speech`. A list of meanings this short and this widely reprinted is not anyone's property. `scripts/coverage.py` checks the lexicon against it by part of speech, so that *walk* as a noun does not stand in for the verb.

`latin-frequency.tsv` lists the 5,000 most frequent lemmas of classical Latin, most frequent first: `rank<TAB>lemma<TAB>count<TAB>part of speech`, then the declension or conjugation, the number of works the lemma occurs in, its LiLa lemma and its commonest forms. `scripts/latin_frequency.py` makes it from the corpus of LASLA (Laboratoire d'Analyse Statistique des Langues Anciennes, Université de Liège): about 1.75 million words of Plautus, Cato, Caesar, Cicero's speeches and three of his treatises, Sallust, Lucretius, Catullus, Vergil, Horace, Tibullus, Propertius, Ovid, one book of Livy, Seneca, Curtius, Petronius, Persius, Juvenal, the two Plinys and Tacitus, every word lemmatised by hand. The corpus is published, linked to the LiLa Lemma Bank, by the LiLa project (CIRCSE, Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore, Milan) at [CIRCSE/LASLA](https://github.com/CIRCSE/LASLA) under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. The corpus itself is not kept here: clone it beside the repository (the script says how). The list and the files in `works/` are derived from it, so they are shared under the same licence, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, with credit to LASLA and to the LiLa project: they may be copied and adapted with that credit and under that licence, and not used commercially. The lemmas are spelt as the corpus spells them (u for u and v, i for i and j, and some older spellings: *quatuor, numus, brachium*); proper names, the adjectives made from them, Greek words and numerals in figures are left out. `scripts/latin_coverage.py` measures the lexicon against the list (`notes/coverage/latin-frequency.md`).

`works/<author>-<work>.tsv` lists the lemmas of one work of the same corpus with their counts in that work, in the same columns (`scripts/latin_frequency.py --work "Vergilius, Eclogae" --tsv refs/works/vergilius-eclogae.tsv`). Vergil's Eclogues are here as a sample; the works and their sizes are listed in `notes/coverage/works.md`. Same source, licence and credit as the frequency list.
